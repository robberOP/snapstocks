01

What Zaggle does, and why it has grown

A B2B2C spend-management platform monetising the corporate expense and rewards workflow — not a pure prepaid-card company.

Incorporated in 2011 and listed in September 2023, Zaggle sells software and payment instruments that sit on top of a corporate's ERP, HRMS and banking rails to automate non-capex spend: employee expenses and reimbursements, tax-saving benefits, vendor payments, and channel/employee rewards. It calls itself India's largest issuer of prepaid cards by volume, with 50 million+ cards issued through 19 bank partners, 3.9 million active users and 3,900+ corporate customers as of March 2026.

Four product pillars

Propel — channel and employee rewards platform (points, gift cards, loyalty). The single largest revenue contributor: 57% of FY26 standalone revenue on a gross basis, but the lowest-margin line because most of that revenue is a pass-through cost of redemption.
Save — employee expense management, digitised reimbursements and tax-benefit administration; the original core product and still the primary cross-sell anchor into new corporate logos.
Zoyer — vendor procure-to-pay and invoice-to-pay with embedded finance; zero revenue at IPO (Sept 2023), now management's highlighted growth engine (the "BROME" module case studies cited on the Q4 call).
Zatix / ZIP / Fleet / Payroll Card — a tail of newer, high-margin, largely software-fee businesses (card analytics, international payments, fleet/OMC spend, payroll cards) that are small today but pitched as the next legs of growth.

The moat, as the company frames it

Three claims recur across the filings and call: (i) network effects — banks, corporates and employees all sit on the same rails, and cross-sell between Save/Propel/Zoyer inside an existing account is the cheapest growth lever the company has; (ii) embedding & switching costs — once Zaggle sits inside a finance team's reconciliation workflow, churn is low (management cites <1.5% customer churn and customer-acquisition cost under 5% of revenue); (iii) scale in card issuance — 50 million+ cards issued gives it negotiating weight with banking/network partners that a smaller SaaS entrant would lack. These are asserted by management rather than independently verified in this dataset, but the <1.5% churn / <5% CAC figures are at least directionally consistent with five years of accelerating, not decelerating, revenue growth off an expanding base.

The structural point the label "SaaS fintech" can obscureOnly ~2% of standalone revenue is a pure software/platform fee. The other 98% is transaction-linked (interchange and gift-card/points redemption), which is why Zaggle reports both a "gross" and a "net" revenue view: on a net basis (after deducting pass-through cost of point redemption), FY26 Adjusted EBITDA margin was 21.7% vs. 9.9% on a gross basis. Both are true; the net number is closer to unit economics, the gross number is what flows through the audited P&L.

Geography, market position, expansion

100% of FY26 revenue was generated within India — the consolidated segment note lists "Outside India: nil" for both the quarter and the year. International expansion is guided, not realised: management describes the UAE/MENA as its "primary growth pillar" but says it currently has no on-ground presence there, citing regional volatility (the war referenced repeatedly on the call) as the reason go-live has slipped from an original June 2026 target to "financial year-end" at the earliest. US entry is being built around the recently acquired DICE IP and enterprise contract base but is also unquantified. Investors should treat both as FY27–28 optionality, not FY27 revenue.

On market position, Zaggle's own claim (from Frost & Sullivan, per the investor presentation) is "#1 spend management company in India" against a ₹1,750 Bn+ (2027E) domestic payments-market opportunity. No independent third-party market-share verification is present in this dataset; this is a company-sourced claim, not an audited fact.

Read left to right, this is a margin trough in FY23–FY25 followed by a recovery that began in FY24 and is still running: PAT margin bottomed at 4.1% in FY23 (the first full year post-scale-up of the low-margin Propel/GTV business) and has rebuilt to 7.2% in FY26, still below the FY22 pre-IPO base of 11.3%. Management's explanation, repeated on the call, is that ESOP costs (₹149.8 Mn in FY24 down to ₹22.3 Mn in FY26) and AI-driven headcount efficiency are now doing the work that scale alone did in the early years.

What moved Q4FY26 specifically

Depreciation & amortisation jumped from ₹61.6 Mn (Q4FY25) to ₹104.1 Mn (Q4FY26) standalone — management attributes this entirely to capitalised technology/product development, which "nearly doubled from ₹30 Cr in H1 to ₹56 Cr in H2 FY26" per the CFO's answer on the call. This is a real cost, just a non-cash one this year.
ESOP cost fell 80% YoY in Q4 (₹12.8 Mn → ₹2.5 Mn), directly boosting the gap between Adjusted and Reported EBITDA growth (45.2% vs. 49.6% YoY) — a genuine, if modest, tailwind that will not repeat at the same rate every quarter.
Other income was ~20–23% of consolidated PBT in both FY25 and FY26 (₹38.0 Cr / ₹185.2 Cr in FY26), almost entirely interest income earned on unutilised IPO/QIP cash. As that cash gets deployed into acquisitions (§08), this tailwind should mechanically shrink — a headwind to watch in FY27 profit quality, not a red flag today.
Finance costs are falling, not rising — ₹76.3 Mn (FY25) → ₹53.3 Mn (FY26) standalone, consistent with a company that repaid debt using QIP proceeds rather than levering up to fund growth.

Margin guidance

No FY27 Adjusted EBITDA margin guidance was given — explicitly deferred because the DICE asset-purchase structure (see §08) now lands integration costs directly on the standalone P&L rather than inside a ring-fenced subsidiary. Management reaffirmed a 5-year path to 14–15% Adjusted EBITDA margin (from 9.9% in FY26) but conceded the ~100bps/year glide path "might go through some temporary variation" in the near term. Read plainly: margin expansion is the guidance most at risk of slipping over the next 2–3 quarters.

Receivables outgrew revenue in FY26Standalone trade receivables rose 55% YoY (₹214.9 Cr → ₹333.9 Cr) against 42% revenue growth — roughly 60 to 66 days sales outstanding. Not yet an alarming absolute level, but this is the single line item an analyst pressed management on directly, and it is the direct mechanical cause of FY26's negative operating cash flow (§06).

Capital allocation: still spending down the IPO/QIP war chest

Zaggle raised ₹362.2 Cr in its September 2023 IPO (fully utilised as of March 2026, per the audited note) and a further ₹574.1 Cr via QIP completed in FY25. Of the QIP proceeds, ₹191.3 Cr had been deployed by March 2026 (debt repayment, strategic investments/acquisitions, general corporate purposes); the remaining ₹382.8 Cr sits in short-term bank deposits and a monitoring account, i.e. this is committed dry powder for further M&A, not yet cash flow generated by the business.

In FY26 the company also raised ₹15.0 Cr via a two-tranche preferential warrant issue (352,734 warrants to the promoter group, 705,467 to non-promoters, at ₹567/warrant with 25% paid up-front) — the promoter group's participation is a mild positive skin-in-the-game signal, though the balance 75% due on conversion is a known future dilution event.

Debt reappeared modestly in FY26 (₹5.1 Cr → ₹43.9 Cr standalone current borrowings) after being paid down to near-zero in FY25; management describes this as short-term, working-capital-linked borrowing "to quickly deploy capital and grow the business" rather than a leverage strategy — consistent with a company still overwhelmingly funded by equity (debt/equity is under 4%).

Capex

Standalone capex rose from ₹67.5 Cr (FY25) to ₹107.1 Cr (FY26), running well ahead of depreciation (₹34.6 Cr) — a genuine expansion of the asset base (largely capitalised software/product development), not maintenance capex. Management declined to give a forward capex number ("difficult to give specifics... but this investment is critical for building market leadership"), which is a reasonable answer for a technology company but also means investors have no explicit ceiling on how much further capitalised development can grow relative to reported profit.

Earnings-quality check: FAILS cleanPAT has grown every year since FY23 (5.8x cumulative, standalone) while consolidated CFO was negative in three of the last four years and free cash flow has been negative in all four. This is not a subtle divergence to tease out of the footnotes — management raised it unprompted on the call, named it the company's top internal priority ("operating cash flow, revenue growth and margin expansion" — in that order), and fielded direct, pointed analyst questions about it (see quotes below). "The reported EBITDA... whatever, ₹50 crores, ₹55 crores of EBITDA that you have every quarter... is that a challenge given the focus now on cash flows that margins can actually see further downside?" — Jayesh Shah, OHM Portfolio Equity Research, Q4FY26 earnings call
"We are just about minus ₹6 crores on negative [standalone operating] cash flow, and we will look forward to improving it to a positive cash flow in the coming quarters... this remains the metric which we continuously track extremely closely." — Dr. Raj Narayanam, Executive Chairman, Q4FY26 earnings call
"Best of luck for next year. Hopefully we'll see a large part of our PAT converted to operating cash flow next year." — Akhil Gulecha, Hornbill Capital, closing an exchange about the Propel business's return on capital

The trajectory within FY26 is genuinely improving on management's own account: standalone OCF moved from roughly −₹33–34 Cr at end-September 2025 to roughly −₹6–7 Cr by end-March 2026 — i.e. quarter-on-quarter progress toward breakeven, even if the annual number is still negative and the consolidated figure (dragged down by newly acquired, "nascent" subsidiaries per management) remains around −₹52 Cr. This dataset does not include a published standalone quarterly cash-flow statement, so that improving trajectory rests on verbal management disclosure rather than an audited table — worth confirming in the Q1FY27 filings due the same day as this note.

Why DICE matters more than its size suggests

DICE had FY25 turnover of just ₹10.85 Cr and was loss-making (management expects it to still be loss-making in FY26). Its significance is structural: the deal was originally a 100% share acquisition (a subsidiary, ring-fenced from the standalone P&L), but was restructured in May 2026 into an asset purchase — meaning DICE's costs and the ~100 transferred employees now sit directly on Zaggle's own standalone P&L. This is the specific reason management gave for withholding FY27 EBITDA-margin guidance until integration completes "in the next few months." It is a small deal with an outsized, temporary effect on near-term margin visibility.

International expansion: guided, then pushed out

US operations were originally targeted to start by June 2026; management confirmed on the call this has slipped "by a couple of quarters," attributed to regional volatility affecting the linked Middle East go-to-market plan, with the DICE acquisition now cited as the platform to relaunch from. UAE/MENA entry has no committed timeline beyond "once peace is restored" in the region. Both remain FY27–28 optionality rather than a funded, dated plan.

Cashback economics: a structural bet on customer habituation

Management was pressed on why prepaid/credit card cashback (currently ~68% of GTV) needs to exist at all, given it consumes cash the company says it wants to conserve. The answer given was explicitly analogous to e-commerce and food-delivery's own historical cashback phase: management expects a "growing category" (spend management, likened to the adoption curves of ERPs, HRMS and CRM before it) to let Zaggle dial down cashback as corporate/consumer habits mature over the guided 4–5 year window — a plausible but unproven thesis that the company is explicitly asking investors to underwrite.

Bottom line

What's good: five straight years of 40–50% revenue growth, three consecutive record quarters into FY26, a clean audit opinion, a debt-light balance sheet sitting on ₹383 Cr of still-undeployed QIP cash, disciplined (not reckless) M&A — including walking away from a deal (EffiaSoft) and renegotiating another (DICE) down 45% — and two acquired subsidiaries (86400, GreenEdge) already growing revenue well over 100% within a year of being bought.

What's not good: free cash flow has been negative for four straight years despite consistently positive and growing PAT; only 2% of revenue is pure high-margin SaaS; FY27 has no EBITDA guidance; two C-suite departures landed in the same ten-day window; and the market has already rendered a harsh verdict — the stock is down more than 50% from its 52-week high, with roughly half of that de-rating having happened before the Q4 results the company is calling its best-ever quarter.

How management has navigated it: credibly, on the evidence here. Rather than deny the cash-flow gap, leadership named it the top internal priority on the call, quantified the quarter-on-quarter standalone improvement (−₹33–34 Cr to −₹6–7 Cr), and is visibly re-pricing the parts of the business (Propel take-rate, cashback ratio) that consume the most working capital. The CFO hire brought in specific M&A/capital-markets pedigree at exactly the moment the company needs to deploy ₹383 Cr of dry powder without repeating past cash-conversion mistakes.

AI-Synthesized ViewLikelihood this resolves in the bulls' favour over the next 12 months: medium, roughly 45–55%. The case rests on whether consolidated operating cash flow actually crosses into positive territory in FY27 — the one metric management has staked its own credibility on and the one this dataset cannot yet confirm, since Q1FY27 results land the same day this note was compiled. A clean, audited quarter of positive consolidated OCF would be the single most re-rating-worthy data point available to this stock; a repeat of FY26's pattern (strong PAT, negative CFO) would likely keep the multiple compressed regardless of how fast the top line grows.
