Equity Research Note · Power Transmission Utility / Regulated Infrastructure (Maharatna CPSE)

Power Grid Corporation of India Limited

NSE: POWERGRID · BSE: 532898 · FY2022–FY2027(Q1) performance review, BSE-filing, investor-call and market-data synthesis, as of 22–23 August 2026.

The one-line story: India's transmission monopoly is sitting on the biggest investment pipeline in its history — ₹1.75 lakh crore of work already in hand, ₹1.19 lakh crore under bidding, and a ₹15 lakh-crore-plus long-run sectoral opportunity through 2036 — and is deploying record capex (₹39,967 crore in FY26 alone) to chase it. But the P&L has not caught up: consolidated Q1 FY27 PAT was flat-to-down year-on-year, standalone PAT fell 7%, EPS has been essentially flat for five years (₹18.09 in FY22 to ₹17.13 in FY26), and return on net worth has slid every single year for five years straight, from 22.1% to 15.9%. Management's own math attributes the latest quarter's stall to a quantified ₹560 crore regulatory-mechanics drag — transparent, but it is also the fourth of the last five quarters in which reported EPS missed Street estimates. Layered on top: a management reshuffle unusually dense even by PSU standards (new Chairman, a Director (Projects) post still on repeatedly-extended additional charge, a CFO seat that changed hands twice in seven weeks, and all three independent directors' terms lapsed without replacement, leaving the board out of compliance with SEBI's own independence norm).

01

What POWERGRID does, and how it has grown

India's sole central transmission utility — a regulated-return monopoly on the wires between generation and distribution, now expanding mainly through competitively-bid subsidiaries rather than its own regulated balance sheet.

Incorporated in 1989 as National Power Transmission Corporation and renamed Power Grid Corporation of India in 1992, POWERGRID owns and operates the backbone of India's interstate power transmission network: 1,84,960 circuit kilometres of lines and 291 substations with 6,24,016 MVA of transformation capacity as of 31 March 2026 (growing to 1,86,595 ckm / 6,34,516 MVA by 30 June 2026, per the Q1 FY27 investor deck). Three reporting segments: Transmission (the overwhelming majority — ~92% of standalone FY26 income), Consultancy (project management, DPR preparation, EV/smart-grid/BESS advisory, with a growing international footprint across ~25 countries), and Telecom (the PowerTel brand — optic-fibre-on-transmission-line connectivity, data-centre and 400G links, plus smart-metering subsidiary PESL, which alone contributed ₹220 crore in Q1 FY27). It is a Maharatna Central Public Sector Enterprise with the Government of India holding 51.34% as of June 2026; credit ratings are AAA domestically (CRISIL, CareEdge, ICRA) and investment-grade internationally, rated "at par with Sovereign" (S&P BBB, Fitch BBB−, Moody's Baa3).

The moat: a regulated monopoly, now overlaid with competitive bidding

Historically POWERGRID built and owned essentially all interstate transmission under the Return-Tariff Mechanism (RTM), where CERC sets Annual Fixed Cost recovery (Return on Equity + Interest on Loan + Depreciation + O&M + Interest on Working Capital) under the CERC (Terms and Conditions of Tariff) Regulations, 2024, applicable to the FY2024–29 tariff block. Since the introduction of Tariff-Based Competitive Bidding (TBCB), new inter-state projects are increasingly auctioned rather than assigned, and POWERGRID's own subsidiaries now compete against private developers (Adani Energy Solutions, Sterlite Power and others) for these awards. As of 30 June 2026, TBCB accounted for 83% of POWERGRID's ₹1.75 lakh crore works-in-hand (RTM only 14%), and the company's cumulative tariff-based market share across all ISTS TBCB projects since the mechanism's inception stands at ~47% per the Q1 FY27 investor deck (44% per the FY26 Annual Report, dated to 31 March 2026 — the two figures differ slightly by vintage and are both reported here rather than reconciled). This is the single most important structural shift in the business model over the past several years: growth increasingly comes from winning competitively-bid SPVs (60 wholly-owned TBCB subsidiaries at FY26-end, expanding further via bolt-on SPV acquisitions in Q1 FY27) rather than automatic RTM allocation, with returns "well-calibrated" per management's own words on the Q1 FY27 call — though, as §03 and §08 detail, an analyst on that same call pointedly asked for TBCB-specific revenue/EBITDA/PAT disclosure that management does not currently provide, a genuine transparency gap that persists as of this report's date.

Read plainly: capex has run at 3.5x its FY22 level by FY26, and transformation capacity (MVA, which better captures HVDC/high-voltage substation additions) has compounded at ~7.1%/year — well ahead of circuit-kilometre growth (~1.6%/year), reflecting a shift toward denser, higher-capacity systems. Yet diluted EPS in FY26 (₹17.13) is barely above FY22's ₹18.09 five years earlier, and return on net worth has fallen in every one of the five years shown, from 22.1% to 15.9% — a genuine, sustained structural decline, not a one-quarter blip. Book value per share has compounded steadily (₹82.0 → ₹108.1, ~7.2%/yr) purely through retained-earnings accretion, which is the main reason the stock has still delivered a rising net-worth base even as per-share profit growth stalled. Section 04 unpacks why: management's own explanation on the Q1 FY27 call is that new capitalisation initially depresses, rather than boosts, near-term reported profit under the regulated-tariff mechanics as older, high-yielding assets roll past their 12-year normative-debt-recovery window.

Management's own bridge: +₹788 Cr of new revenue, netted down to roughly flat PAT by a ₹560 Cr regulatory dragOn the Q1FY27 call, the CMD walked through the exact mechanics: new asset commissioning should have added ₹788 Cr of transmission-charge revenue and ~₹247 Cr of PAT year-on-year. Against that, two items subtracted about ₹560 Cr: (1) ~₹330 Cr from the regulator's tariff design itself — CERC recognises cost-of-debt/depreciation and "unwinds" that recovery over 12 years, so tariff (and hence revenue) on assets crossing that vintage mechanically declines; and (2) ~₹230 Cr from a shrinking "interest on differential tariff" item — POWERGRID earns SBI MCLR+150bps on revenue accrued between filing a tariff petition and CERC's final order, and this was unusually large in Q1FY26 as a backlog of orders was still pending, but has since normalised as CERC caught up. A further ₹33 Cr one-off write-back (KSK Mahanadi) present in the year-ago quarter did not repeat. Management was explicit that capitalisation of ~12-year-old vintage assets (from the 2014–16 capex cycle) will keep producing this kind of drag going forward — "that is the characteristic of the industry itself" — framing it as structural, not one-off.

Analysts are not fully satisfied with this explanation

On the same call, an analyst (Avendus Spark) pointed out that despite ₹28,000 Cr of consolidated capitalisation in the prior year, profits have "remained largely flat," and another (Ambit Capital) pressed on whether the drag should even be PAT-neutral in principle (revenue and expense both fall together under the regulated formula) — management's answer conceded there are "marginal" mismatches between notional/regulatory depreciation-interest and actual accounted figures, without fully quantifying them. A third analyst (Avendus/Ambit-adjacent, ICICI-hosted call) explicitly asked for a standalone TBCB-only P&L disclosure (revenue/EBITDA/PAT), since TBCB SPVs are now the primary growth driver; the CMD acknowledged the concern ("I do appreciate the concern... we will examine that") but did not commit to a specific new disclosure or timeline. Until that exists, outside analysts cannot independently verify whether TBCB-project-level returns are holding up as management asserts.

Segment margin: Telecom weakening, Transmission broadly flat

Consolidated segment PBIT, Q1FY27 vs Q1FY26: Transmission +0.5% (₹6,361 Cr vs ₹6,328 Cr, essentially flat despite +2.2% revenue — i.e. margin compression within the segment), Telecom −30.8% (₹98 Cr vs ₹142 Cr, on revenue down 13.0%), Consultancy roughly flat (₹30.1 Cr vs ₹30.6 Cr despite +27.8% revenue growth, implying dilutive mix). Receivables: management's own metric says "improving," the regulatory ratio disclosure says the opposite

The Q1FY27 investor deck highlights "Robust Collections Continue": billing of ₹10,963 Cr against realisation of ₹11,404 Cr (104.03% realisation rate), with billing-basis receivable days falling sharply from 19.41 (Q1FY26) to 12.11 (Q1FY27) — management called this out explicitly on the call as a positive. Yet the company's own regulatory ratio table in the same financial-results filing shows the standard Debtors Turnover ratio (annualised revenue ÷ average trade receivables) declining over the same period — standalone 4.96x (Q1FY26) → 3.77x (Q4FY26) → 3.86x (Q1FY27); consolidated 5.06x → 3.97x → 4.15x — which on its face implies receivable days lengthened, not shortened, over the same year. These are two different receivables metrics (billing-window days vs. a turnover ratio using average trade receivables) measuring different things, and this report cannot fully reconcile them from the disclosed data alone; it is flagged here as a genuine discrepancy between management's own headline framing and the regulatory-disclosure ratio sitting in the same filing, worth independently verifying against the next quarter's balance sheet receivables figure once available.

Capex plan and funding: record spend, funded increasingly by debt

FY26 capex (accrual basis) was ₹39,967 Cr, the company's highest ever in a single year, but capitalisation was only ₹28,206 Cr — an ~₹11,760 Cr gap that shows up directly as a 33% jump in capital-work-in-progress (₹36,024 Cr → ₹47,888 Cr, FY25→FY26). FY27 guidance is ₹37,000 Cr capex / ₹30,000 Cr capitalisation (IR deck) against a wider ₹40,000–45,000 Cr/year FY27–29 planning band disclosed separately in the AGM notice (§03). To fund this, shareholders approved raising the Board's overall borrowing ceiling from ₹1.80 lakh crore to ₹2.20 lakh crore (postal-ballot outcome, 20 Aug 2026) and domestic bond private-placement limits to ₹35,000 Cr for FY27–28; total outstanding borrowings stood at ₹1,44,556 Cr as of 31 March 2026 per the AGM notice, projected to require ~₹1,09,200 Cr of fresh loan tie-ups over FY27–29 net of ₹51,072 Cr of scheduled repayments. On the Q1FY27 call, management confirmed that the ₹13,000 Cr+ of equity invested in TBCB projects (operational + under-construction) to date has been funded entirely through internal accruals, not incremental parent-level debt — a genuinely reassuring detail on capital-structure discipline at the holding-company level, even as consolidated group debt continues climbing to fund the SPVs themselves.

Related-party structure: dominated by the TBCB subsidiary web, not promoter-style dealings

The FY26 Annual Report's related-party disclosure (Note 55) runs many pages, but this reflects the sheer count of wholly-owned TBCB subsidiaries (60 at FY26-end, growing) rather than related-party red flags of the kind seen in private-sector governance failures — equity infusions into and O&M service agreements with wholly-owned SPVs are structurally normal for this business model and are, by definition, consolidated away at the group level. This report did not extract a specific aggregate contingent-liability rupee figure from the annual report notes within the time available; that is flagged here as a specific data gap rather than filled with an estimate.

Regulated-return business model: what it means for governance and returns

As a Maharatna CPSE with the Government of India holding 51.34%, POWERGRID's Chairman, functional directors and (nominally) independent directors are all appointed through the government's administrative process rather than an independent nomination committee in the conventional listed-company sense — a structural feature of every GoI-controlled utility, not unique to POWERGRID, but one that the vacancy pattern above makes concretely visible. On the regulatory side, the CERC (Terms and Conditions of Tariff) Regulations, 2024 fix the Return-on-Equity component of Annual Fixed Cost recovery for the FY2024–29 tariff block on RTM assets, while TBCB assets earn whatever return the company itself bid to accept in a competitive auction — meaning incremental growth increasingly depends on POWERGRID's own bid discipline (management describes "well-calibrated" returns, §01) rather than an administratively-fixed RoE, a genuine shift in the risk/return character of new capex versus the legacy RTM asset base.

Other conference-call insights worth watching

Equipment-supply easing, an active BESS regulatory push, and an unresolved transparency ask on TBCB-level disclosure — each with the potential to move the stock independently of the headline P&L.

Equipment-supply constraints reported easing, prices "stabilising"

Multiple analysts pressed on transformer and GIS-equipment supply bottlenecks that have plagued the transmission sector; management's response was that OEM capacity has been ramping (helped by POWERGRID giving manufacturers bulk, multi-project procurement visibility rather than project-by-project orders) and that recently-approved Chinese-OEM participation (4 companies cleared by the Government of India) should further ease supply. Management characterised prices as "quite stabilised" with room to improve project economics if raw-material costs cooperate — a genuine tailwind for capex execution pace if it holds, though it is management's own characterisation and not independently corroborated in this dataset.

POWERGRID moving into BESS under the regulated (Section 62) route

Following a March 2026 CERC tariff-regulation amendment permitting transmission licensees to develop integrated (battery) storage systems, POWERGRID has already obtained Northern and Western Regional Power Committee consent and filed petitions with the regulator to enter the BESS business under regulated-return terms — a new, potentially significant growth avenue layered on top of the FY26 first-BESS-project win (Kalikiri, TBCB route). No timeline or capex quantum was given; management said only that it will "mature" as the regulator and other stakeholders respond.

Revenue is de-linked from actual power flow — a direct answer to a renewables-underutilisation concern

Asked whether transmission lines built for renewable-energy evacuation sit underutilised (and dues become hard to collect) when generation projects lag, management was explicit that POWERGRID's tariff is a fixed annual charge, not a per-unit-of-power charge — revenue accrues once an asset is commissioned and deemed operational (DOCO), independent of whether the associated generator has actually come online. About ₹300 Cr of revenue was cited as pending recognition specifically because regulatory clearance for deemed-DOCO status on certain RTM assets is still in process — a small, quantified, near-term revenue item to watch land in coming quarters.

TBCB-level disclosure: acknowledged gap, no commitment

As covered in §04, an analyst's explicit request for standalone TBCB revenue/EBITDA/PAT disclosure — given TBCB is now the primary growth driver — received an acknowledgement of the concern but no firm commitment to new disclosure. This is worth flagging as a recurring engagement point likely to resurface on subsequent calls, and a genuine limit on how precisely outside analysts can currently model the return profile of the TBCB SPV book that increasingly drives consolidated results.

HVDC pipeline: ~21 projects under evaluation across multiple programmes

Management quantified a long-run HVDC opportunity of ~21 projects (14 from the CEA's 900+ GW plan, 6 from the Brahmaputra basin hydro outlook, plus international links to Sri Lanka/Andaman/Myanmar), noting HVDC's long gestation and high unit cost mean planners weigh it carefully against BESS and 1,200kV HVAC alternatives project-by-project rather than defaulting to it.

Bottom line

What's good: an unmatched, structurally protected position as India's dominant interstate transmission utility, with a AAA domestic credit profile, a genuinely enormous and growing order book (₹1.75 lakh Cr in hand, ₹1.19 lakh Cr under bidding, >₹15 lakh Cr of long-run sectoral opportunity through 2036), record capex execution (₹39,967 Cr in FY26, an all-time high), first-mover entries into new asset classes (BESS, Synchronous Condensers, solar generation), clean CFO/PAT conversion every year for the last four (2.3–2.6x), internal-accruals-only funding of TBCB equity investment to date, a 4.50% trailing dividend yield, an unmodified audit opinion, and a consistent "Excellent" MoU performance rating stretching back to 1993-94.

What's not good: return on net worth has declined every single year for five straight years (22.1% → 15.9%), diluted EPS in FY26 is barely above its FY22 level, four of the last five reported quarters missed consensus EPS (the exception being a one-off tax-driven beat), the current ratio has fallen for four consecutive quarters to 0.57x, free cash flow collapsed to near-zero in FY26 as capex absorbed essentially all operating cash flow, a ₹11,760 Cr capex-to-capitalisation gap in FY26 alone is visibly swelling capital-work-in-progress, management's own receivables-improvement claim is contradicted by the regulatory turnover-ratio disclosure in the very same filing, an analyst's explicit request for TBCB-level P&L transparency went unanswered beyond an acknowledgement, and the leadership bench has churned unusually hard — a new CMD, a Director (Projects) seat on repeatedly-extended additional charge, a CFO who lasted seven weeks, and a board that has been out of compliance with SEBI's independent-director quota since mid-April 2026 with no replacement confirmed in this dataset.

How management has navigated it: transparently on the mechanics, less so on the disclosure depth. The CMD walked analysts through a precise, quantified bridge (+₹788 Cr revenue, −₹560 Cr regulatory drag, netting to roughly flat PAT) rather than deflecting the flat-profit question, and was candid that this dynamic is structural rather than one-off. But the same call surfaced an unresolved ask for TBCB-level segment disclosure that management has "appreciated" without committing to, and separate filings show a genuinely unusual concentration of near-simultaneous leadership transitions for a company of this scale and importance, without much public explanation for the CFO's unusually short seven-week tenure or the still-unfilled Director (Projects) role.

AI-Synthesized ViewLikelihood the next 12 months resolve in the bulls' favour from today's starting price: moderate, roughly 40–50%. The order-book and capex story is real, well-documented, and arguably underappreciated at 2.52x book against a AAA-rated, structurally protected monopoly — but this report's own tabulated numbers show a five-year RoE decline that has not yet troughed, a run of consensus EPS misses that predates and continues through the most recent quarter, tightening working-capital ratios, and a governance picture with more open vacancies than is comfortable for a company of this systemic importance. The dividend yield provides a floor to total return that the pure earnings story does not yet support; whether the stock re-rates meaningfully higher likely depends on capitalisation genuinely catching up to capex, and on RoE finding a bottom, both of which remain open questions as of this report's date rather than settled positives.
