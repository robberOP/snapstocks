Equity Research Note · Renewable Power Generation (Solar / Wind / Storage) · PSU-Promoted

NTPC Green Energy Limited

NSE / BSE: NTPCGREEN (544289) · FY2023–FY2027(Q1) performance review, BSE-filing, Annual Report and press-release synthesis, as of 22 August 2026.

The one-line story: NGEL rode a genuine, well-documented capacity build — installed capacity up 70.7% YoY to 10,076 MW, generation up 111.5% — to 29.4% consolidated revenue growth in FY26, but the growth is lumpier and more leveraged than the headline suggests: capacity utilisation actually fell (24.07%→22.48%) on weather and grid curtailment, a joint-venture equity-method loss knocked one quarter's (Dec-25) net margin down to just 2.65%, other income collapsed −31% as IPO-deposit interest ran out, finance costs and depreciation are compounding faster than revenue, and standalone (parent-only) profit actually fell 17.0% even as consolidated profit rose 10.0% on subsidiary/JV consolidation. Debt/equity nearly doubled to 1.54x against a further ₹5,000 crore FY27 borrowing authorisation and a ₹24,391 crore capital commitment already on the books. No dividend was paid, and none is planned while the capex programme runs. The stock trades at 4.1x book and 147x trailing earnings for a business currently generating roughly a 2.8% return on equity, with the Government of India/NTPC still holding about 89% of the company and only ~11% of shares in free float.

01

What NGEL does, and how it has grown

The dedicated renewable-energy arm of NTPC Limited — India's largest power generator — built almost entirely since incorporation in 2022, now sitting on a 26,544 MW portfolio just four years later.

NTPC Green Energy Limited (NGEL) was incorporated in April 2022 as the renewable-energy vehicle of the NTPC Group, listed via a ₹10,000 crore fresh-issue IPO that completed in FY2024-25 (92,63,29,669 new equity shares at ₹10 face value; proceeds fully utilised by 30 September 2025 per the company's own financial-results notes). It develops, builds, owns and operates utility-scale solar, wind, hybrid, battery-storage (BESS), pumped-storage and firm-and-dispatchable renewable energy (FDRE) projects across India, and separately earns a small consultancy/project-management/supervision fee stream (₹21.99 Cr in FY26, consolidated — MD&A). The Company is run lean: MarketData lists just 261 full-time employees for a business that closed FY26 with a ₹60,382 Cr consolidated balance sheet — consistent with an asset-owning developer that relies on EPC contractors and subsidiaries/JVs rather than an in-house construction workforce.

FY26 capacity additions were 4,174 MW versus 2,977 MW in FY25 (+40.21% YoY), and generation rose to 14,596 million units (MU) from 6,901 MU (+111.51% YoY) — both figures sourced directly from the Annual Report's "Key Operational Highlights" table. Major projects driving this include the Khavda and Bhadla solar parks (Gujarat/Rajasthan) and the Pudimadaka Green Hydrogen Hub (Andhra Pradesh). Section 03 shows that this capacity growth converted to revenue growth, but that a falling capacity-utilisation factor (CUF) meant the conversion was less efficient in FY26 than FY25 — a nuance the MW-growth headline alone would miss.

The moat: NTPC parentage, project-execution scale, and a AAA balance sheet — not a technology edge

No independent market-share ranking specific to NGEL was found in any reviewed filing; the Annual Report cites India's move to 3rd globally in installed renewable capacity (IRENA, Dec-2025 data) as sector context, not a company-specific claim, and this report treats it as such. What is independently verifiable: NGEL's non-convertible debentures and long-term loans carry the highest domestic ratings (CRISIL AAA/Stable, IND AAA/Stable) — a genuine, hard-to-replicate funding-cost advantage flowing directly from NTPC/Government of India parentage; access to central and state counterparties (SECI, state DISCOMs, state gencos) through a partnership-led JV model (six subsidiaries, four joint ventures as at 31 March 2026); and a demonstrated ability to win competitive tenders across solar, wind and now battery storage (Section 03). Whether this adds up to a durable competitive moat versus other well-capitalised renewable developers (Adani Green, Tata Power Renewables, ReNew, JSW Energy et al.) is not something this dataset can independently benchmark — no peer-comparison data was available in the sources reviewed.

Diversification beyond plain-vanilla solar/wind

The FY26 Annual Report describes several new-business initiatives still at an early, non-revenue-generating stage: a 100 MWh Vanadium Redox Flow Battery (VRFB) pilot at Khavda (via subsidiary NTPC Renewable Energy Ltd); a 100 MW Agri-Photovoltaic (AgriPV) EPC tender; and an expanding Commercial & Industrial (C&I) push targeting data-centre demand, evidenced by MoUs with CtrlS Data Centre (up to 2 GW, 31 Oct 2025) and Nxtra Data Limited (up to 500 MW, 24 Mar 2026) plus a signed 1,200 MW bilateral solar PPA with PTC India Limited (3 Jul 2026, Section 03). None of these are yet reflected in the P&L in any quantifiable way in this dataset — they are pipeline/optionality, not delivered revenue, and this report treats them accordingly.

Geography, segments and expansion

NGEL discloses no segment breakdown: the Annual Report states plainly it "does not have any separate reportable segments in accordance with Ind AS 108" because it is "engaged primarily in the business of power generation from renewable energy sources" — a single-segment business by the company's own accounting treatment, which means no revenue-by-technology (solar vs wind vs storage) or revenue-by-state split exists in any filing reviewed. All disclosed projects are domestic (India); no export revenue or overseas project was found. Horizontal expansion is real: from solar/wind generation into standalone battery storage (BESS), FDRE/peak-power products, and early C&I/data-centre offtake. Vertical/new-technology expansion is nascent: VRFB storage and AgriPV are pilot-stage per the Annual Report's own description, not yet revenue-contributing.

Operating profit is growing in line with capacity — but nearly all of that growth is being absorbed below the EBITDA lineOperating EBITDA grew a healthy 29.13% in FY26, essentially matching capacity growth, and Operating EBITDA margin held almost flat (86.74%→86.59%) — the core generation business itself is not deteriorating. This is a classic capital-intensive build-out signature, not obviously a red flag on its own, but it does mean profit growth will keep lagging capacity growth for as long as the capex programme (Section 05) keeps adding to the depreciation and interest base faster than new capacity generates incremental EBITDA.

YoY margin compression shows up even in the strongest quarter

Q1FY27 posted the highest quarterly consolidated PAT in this dataset (₹304.84 Cr, EPS ₹0.36, +38.5% YoY per MarketData's own earnings-growth figure) — a genuinely strong quarter in absolute terms. Yet on a margin basis it was weaker than the prior-year comparable: consolidated operating margin fell from 69.05% (Q1FY26) to 62.32% (Q1FY27), and net margin fell from 32.41% to 27.54%, even as the standalone entity's own figures moved the same way (net margin 29.49%→26.78%). The absolute profit grew because revenue grew faster than margin compressed — but a reader looking only at the "+38.5% earnings growth" headline would miss that per-rupee-of-revenue profitability is trending down, driven by the same finance-cost/depreciation dynamic as the FY26 annual numbers: standalone finance costs alone rose from ₹164.23 Cr (Q1FY26) to ₹154.66 Cr... (consolidated finance cost Q1FY27 ₹321.51 Cr vs Q1FY26 ₹192.56 Cr, +67% YoY) — consolidated finance costs are rising considerably faster than consolidated revenue (+67% vs +62.7%), a gap this report flags as worth monitoring rather than dismisses.

The gap is stark: ₹24,391 Cr of PPE contractually committed, against a single, newly-approved ₹5,000 Cr FY27 NCD/bond authorisation — roughly one-fifth of the committed spend. This is not necessarily alarming on its own (the company also draws term loans and other bank facilities not separately quantified in this authorisation, and FY26's actual debt issuance per the cash-flow statement was ₹15,731.88 Cr, well above the FY26-equivalent ₹1,500 Cr NCD figure, implying most funding comes through channels this specific board resolution doesn't capture) — but it does mean the ₹5,000 Cr figure by itself materially understates the total external funding NGEL's committed pipeline will require over the coming 1–2 years, and this report found no single filing that quantifies total planned FY27/FY28 funding needs end-to-end.

Dividend policy: explicitly suspended for the capex build-out

NGEL paid no dividend in FY26 (or, per the Annual Report, in any year since incorporation), and states the reason plainly: "Company's subsidiaries and joint ventures have significant capital expenditure (capex) plans that will require substantial equity infusion in the coming years. Keeping in view of the same, no dividend is proposed for FY 2025-26." The Annual Report separately flags this as a deviation from the DPE (Department of Public Enterprises) Guidelines on Corporate Governance for CPSEs, which ordinarily expect a dividend from profitable central PSUs — an explicitly disclosed, reasoned exception rather than a silent lapse, but still a real opportunity cost for shareholders relative to peer PSUs that do pay.

Related-party transactions and contingent liabilities: both immaterial in scale

Disclosed FY26 related-party transactions with holding company NTPC Ltd were modest relative to the balance sheet: sale of goods/services of ₹35.32 Cr and purchases of ₹0.08 Cr (Ind AS 24 note), alongside a ₹11.99 Cr advance to NTPC GE Power Services Pvt Ltd (a JV of the holding company, down from ₹60.44 Cr in FY25). None of this rises to a scale (relative to ₹2,858 Cr consolidated revenue) that this report would flag as a governance concern on its own; see §07 for the separate, more consequential related-party dynamic (shared KMP between NGEL and parent NTPC Ltd). Contingent liabilities are a single, unquantifiable Power Grid Corporation transmission-charges dispute that management judges immaterial; contingent assets (change-in-law compensation claims for safeguard duty/GST increases) total an estimated ₹91.44 Cr not yet recognised as revenue — a modest potential upside, not a risk.

Auditor: clean opinion, both statements, both quarters reviewed

Statutory auditor P.R. Mehra & Co. issued an unmodified/clean opinion on both standalone and consolidated FY26 annual financial statements (audit) and on both standalone and consolidated Q1FY27 results (limited review) — no qualification, emphasis of matter, or auditor change found anywhere in this dataset. The FY26 audited accounts remain subject to review by the Comptroller and Auditor General of India under Section 143(6) of the Companies Act, standard for a Government-controlled entity.

Earnings-quality check: passes clean, and unusually wellCFO/PAT conversion is 4.31x–4.57x, well above the 1.0x baseline this report's methodology treats as a floor for healthy conversion. This is not a red flag in NGEL's case — it is the expected signature of a capital-intensive generation business where depreciation (₹1,101.95 Cr FY26) is a very large non-cash charge that depresses reported PAT well below actual cash generated. If anything, this is a genuine positive: the underlying generation assets are throwing off substantially more cash than the accounting profit line suggests, and CFO itself grew 16.3% YoY, comfortably ahead of PAT's 9.9% growth — cash generation is not lagging the profit trend, it is running ahead of it.
⚠
Free cash flow is deeply negative and getting more so — by construction, not by problemFCF was −₹9,986 Cr in FY25 and −₹12,880 Cr in FY26, as capex (₹15,264 Cr FY26, +26.8% YoY) continues to run nearly 14x depreciation — unambiguous evidence of real, ongoing capacity expansion (Section 01), not asset-stripping or maintenance-only spend. But it also means NGEL is nowhere near self-funding: every year's capex gap has been bridged by financing cash flow (+₹9,796 Cr FY26, +₹15,715 Cr FY25) — increasingly debt rather than the FY25 IPO-equity-driven mix (Section 05). Until capacity additions decelerate or CFO scales enough to cover capex on its own, the business will remain externally funded, and its cost of that funding (finance costs, already growing faster than revenue per §04) is the single biggest swing factor in future profit.

Management commentary on cash generation: the Annual Report's only relevant statement is brief: "Despite higher borrowing levels, strong operating cash flows enabled comfortable servicing of debt obligations." This is consistent with the debt-service coverage ratio improving from 1.38x to 1.58x FY25→FY26 (Section 04) — a genuine, verifiable claim, though DSCR above 1.0x but below ~2x still leaves relatively thin headroom for a company simultaneously planning to add another ₹24,391 Cr of committed capex (Section 05).

Other disclosure insights

With no earnings call or investor deck to draw on, the most informative signals in this dataset come from the pattern and pace of BSE filings themselves.

A genuinely diversifying counterparty base

The FY26/Q1FY27 order-win filings (Section 03) span four different tender counterparties — SECI (central), WBSEDCL and MPPMCL (state DISCOMs), and a bilateral PPA with PTC India — plus a unique defence-land project directly approved by the Union Defence Minister. This spread across central-tender, state-DISCOM and bilateral-PPA revenue streams is a genuine diversification of offtake counterparty risk, evidenced directly rather than merely claimed by management, though this report has no data on customer concentration (e.g., what % of FY26 actual billed revenue came from SECI vs state DISCOMs) to quantify how diversified the existing operating book already is.

The CtrlS/data-centre JV: optionality, not yet revenue

The 22 May 2026 board meeting that approved FY26 results also approved incorporating a joint venture with CtrlS Datacenters Limited, and the Annual Report separately lists MoUs with Nxtra Data and ENEOS Corporation (green methanol/hydrogen). These position NGEL for the data-centre power-demand theme increasingly visible in Indian power-sector commentary, but every one of these remains at MoU/incorporation stage in this dataset — none has a disclosed capacity, tariff or revenue-start date, and this report treats them as pipeline optionality only.

Filing cadence and completeness

Of the 49 filings in this dataset, roughly half are press releases or "General" operational-milestone disclosures (commercial-operation-date declarations for specific project tranches) rather than financial or governance filings — a high-frequency disclosure cadence consistent with an actively commissioning developer, and one that gives this report reasonable confidence in the completeness of the capacity-addition narrative in Section 01, even though the financial-results filing history itself is short (Section 03's coverage note).

Bottom line

What's good: a genuinely large, verifiable capacity build (operational capacity +70.7% YoY to 10,076 MW, generation +111.5%) backed by AAA-rated debt and NTPC/Government parentage; core Operating EBITDA margins near 87% and essentially flat despite the scale-up, showing the underlying generation business is not deteriorating; operating cash flow that is growing faster than reported profit and converting at 4.3–4.6x PAT, a genuinely clean earnings-quality signature for a capital-intensive business; unmodified/clean audit opinions on every financial statement reviewed; stable CFO tenure through the IPO and scale-up period; a diversifying counterparty base (SECI, multiple state DISCOMs, a bilateral PPA, a unique defence-land project) and early, credible optionality in storage, FDRE and data-centre-driven C&I demand; and a ₹24,391 Cr committed capital pipeline that provides multi-year revenue visibility if executed on schedule.

What's not good: capacity utilisation actually fell in FY26 (24.07%→22.48%) on weather/curtailment, meaning revenue growth partly outran actual generation efficiency; a joint-venture equity-method loss (−₹31.95 Cr) drove one quarter's (Dec-25) net margin down to just 2.65%, the kind of single-quarter shock this report has no forward visibility into recurring or not; the standalone parent entity's own revenue and profit both declined in FY26 (−2.76%, −17.02%) even as consolidated headlines rose, meaning all growth currently sits inside subsidiaries/JVs where NGEL shareholders capture only a proportionate, sometimes-volatile share; other income (largely IPO-deposit interest) fell 31% as that one-off tailwind expired; finance costs and depreciation are both compounding faster than revenue, capping PAT growth well below capacity growth; debt/equity nearly doubled to 1.54x against a committed capex bill nearly 5x the newly authorised FY27 borrowing; free cash flow is deeply negative and widening (−₹12,880 Cr FY26); no dividend has been or will be paid while the capex programme runs; three independent directors turned over entirely within six months of appointment with no stated reason found in this dataset; and the company's top three executive roles are held on additional charge by NTPC Limited officers, a standard but real minority-alignment consideration given the Government/NTPC's ~89% ownership.

How management has navigated it: operationally, competently and verifiably — the capacity-addition and generation figures in the Annual Report are specific, consistent with the flow of commercial-operation-date filings throughout the year, and cross-check cleanly against MarketData's independently-pulled figures. On disclosure, management has been transparent about the things it chooses to disclose (the CUF decline, the dividend-suspension rationale, the DPE-guideline deviation are all stated plainly rather than buried), but there is no channel in this dataset — no earnings call, no investor deck — through which management explains the Q3FY26 margin shock, the standalone-vs-consolidated divergence, or gives the market any numeric forward guidance to hold it to. That absence is itself the most actionable governance observation in this report: for a ₹77,000+ Cr market-cap, recently-IPO'd company, the lack of a standing quarterly analyst-engagement channel in the sources reviewed is unusual and worth the reader independently verifying (it may simply be a gap in this specific data-collection pipeline rather than in NGEL's actual investor-relations practice).

AI-Synthesized ViewLikelihood the next 12 months resolve in the bulls' favour from today's starting price: roughly even odds, 40–50%. The capacity-growth story is real and independently verifiable, the balance sheet carries the highest domestic credit rating, and cash generation is genuinely ahead of reported profit — but this report's own tabulated numbers show a business whose profit growth is structurally capped by rising finance costs and depreciation for as long as the capex programme runs, whose consolidated headline growth is not matched at the standalone parent level, and whose quarterly earnings can swing sharply on a single JV's weather-driven performance. None of these are fatal for a young, capex-heavy PSU renewable developer scaling into a genuinely large committed pipeline — but they are exactly the kind of dynamics a standing analyst call would normally probe management on every quarter, and this dataset shows no evidence that channel currently exists.
