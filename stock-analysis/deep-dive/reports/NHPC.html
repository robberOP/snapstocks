Equity Research Note · Integrated Power Utility (Hydro / Solar / Wind) · Central PSU

NHPC Limited

NSE: NHPC · BSE: 533098 · FY2023–FY2027(Q1) performance review, BSE-filing and earnings-call synthesis, as of 22 August 2026.

The one-line story: NHPC is mid-way through the biggest capacity build-out in its 50-year history — Parbati-II and part of Subansiri Lower now commissioned, Pakal Dul/Kiru due within a year, Kamala and Etalin freshly approved — and FY26 revenue grew 11.9% on that new capacity. But headline FY26 PAT growth of 25% was flattered by a roughly ₹900 crore one-off deferred-tax credit that management itself calls out as an adjustment; strip it out and core profit likely fell versus FY25. Operating cash flow fell 34% even as reported profit rose, plant availability slipped to 74.75% (partly because Teesta-V sat fully offline the entire year), ₹450 crore of revenue from the two newest, biggest assets remains unbooked pending CERC tariff orders, debt is up 26% YoY funding an unfinanced capex pipeline north of ₹1.3 lakh crore across disclosed projects, and a fatal tunnel explosion at the under-construction Teesta-VI project (10 dead, 20 July 2026) lands right as the government sold up to 6% of its own stake via OFS. The growth story is real; so is the list of things that have to go right for it to show up in per-share earnings.

01

What NHPC does, and how it has grown

India's largest hydropower generator by installed capacity — a 50-year-old, majority-government-owned utility now mid-way through the biggest capacity build cycle in its history.

NHPC Limited (CIN L40101HR1975GOI032564), incorporated in 1975 and headquartered in Faridabad, Haryana, is a Government of India "Navratna" enterprise engaged in generation, sale and trading of electricity from hydro, wind and solar power stations across India and Nepal, alongside consultancy, project-management, design-and-engineering and O&M services for third-party power projects (MarketData/overview.md business summary). Electricity generation is the company's only reportable segment per its own financial-results notes — power trading, contracts, project management and consultancy are all sub-scale and undisclosed as separate segments — and all power stations sit within a single geographical segment (India). As of early August 2026 the group's operating capacity stood at 7,651 MW following a run of commissionings over the past year (Company press release, 5 Aug 2026): Parbati-II (800 MW), four of eight units (1,000 MW) of the 2,000 MW Subansiri Lower project, Karnisar solar (300 MW, Bikaner), and the restored 510 MW Teesta-V station (fully back only from July 2026, after being offline the whole of FY26 — see §03).

The regulated-return moat

NHPC operates almost entirely under CERC's cost-plus regulated-tariff framework (Tariff Regulations for the period April 2024–March 2029, notified 15 March 2024): customers are billed against an Annual Fixed Charge (AFC) set by the regulator, and construction-cost increases beyond management's control are explicitly pass-through — "whatever cost beyond our control, that is definitely allowed by the CERC," per Director (Finance) Mahesh Kumar Sharma on the Q4FY26 call. This is the core of the investment case: a growing base of "regulated equity" (management's term for the capital base on which the AFC/return is calculated) compounds into growing, contractually-underwritten cash flow as new hydro capacity is commissioned, largely insulated from merchant-power price volatility. Management quantified this directly on the call: regulated equity including Parbati-II stood at ₹18,309 crore as of the FY26 call, and is guided to reach ₹30,672 crore (hydro only) by end-FY27 as Subansiri Lower's remaining 1,000 MW, Rangit-IV (120 MW), Pakal Dul (1,000 MW) and Kiru (624 MW) come online — a 67% increase in the regulated capital base within roughly a year, before the ~1,190 MW of solar assets due in the same window are even added.

Corporate structure and scale of the pipeline

The consolidated group comprises NHPC Limited plus seven subsidiaries (NHDC Limited 51.08% owned, Chenab Valley Power Projects Ltd 50.86%, Loktak Downstream Hydroelectric Corp 74%, Bundelkhand Saur Urja Ltd 90.63%, Jalpower Corporation Ltd 100% — mid-merger into the parent, see §07, Ratle Hydroelectric Power Corp 51%, NHPC Renewable Energy Ltd 100%), one joint venture (APGENCO NHPC Green Energy Ltd, 50%) and one associate (National High Power Test Laboratory, 12.5%) — per the Q1FY27 consolidated results notes. Beyond the currently-commissioning projects, management's own Q4FY26 call disclosed a pipeline whose disclosed estimated project costs sum to roughly ₹1.3 lakh crore: Subansiri Lower (₹30,072 Cr total, ₹26,092 Cr incurred to Mar-26), Dibang Multipurpose 2,880 MW (₹31,876 Cr, incl. ₹6,716 Cr flood-moderation grant, ₹4,504 Cr incurred, completion Feb-2032), Kamala HE 1,720 MW in Arunachal Pradesh (₹26,070 Cr, CCEA-approved April 2026, to be built via a new JV with the Arunachal Pradesh government), Teesta-VI 500 MW (₹9,167 Cr, 72% physically complete — site of the July 2026 tunnel explosion, §02/§07), Pakal Dul 1,000 MW and Kiru 624 MW (₹12,728 Cr and ₹5,409 Cr, both 80%+ complete, targeted Q4FY27), Kwar 540 MW (₹4,526 Cr, 33% complete), Ratle 850 MW (₹5,282 Cr, only 29% complete), Uri-I Stage-II and Dulhasti Stage-II (₹2,709 Cr / ₹2,994 Cr, board-approved Feb-2026, completion Nov-2029), plus a freshly signed (16 Jun 2026) Memorandum of Agreement with the Arunachal Pradesh government for the 3,097 MW Etalin project on a 40-year Build-Own-Operate-Transfer basis — still at the pre-feasibility stage and not yet costed. Against this, current net worth is roughly ₹42,500 crore and market capitalisation ₹79,600 crore (§05, §10): the growth pipeline dwarfs the balance sheet that has to fund it.

How the moat is evidenced, and where it isn't

No independently sourced market-share or competitor-ranking data was found in this dataset; NHPC's own materials describe it as India's largest hydropower generator, which is consistent with the scale of assets and pipeline disclosed above but is not independently benchmarked here. The moat itself — regulated cost-plus tariffs, government ownership giving privileged access to inter-state river-basin allocations and long project-gestation land/forest clearances — is real and structural, but it comes with the flip side documented through this report: multi-year execution timelines, tariff orders that lag commissioning by months to years (§03), and safety/execution risk inherent to Himalayan tunnelling (§02, §07).

The swing from a weak Q3FY26 (EBITDA margin ~22% on the yfinance basis) to a strong Q4FY26 and Q1FY27 lines up with the operational narrative management gave on the call: Dulhasti, Chamera-III and TLDP-IV underwent MIV-repair and gate-repair shutdowns during FY26, and TLDP-III/IV specifically carried silt damage traced back to the October 2023 Teesta-V flash floods, which management said on 18 May 2026 it expected to clear "within three months." Full-year FY26 generation reached 29,619 million units, up ~16% YoY (25,548 MU in FY25), but the group's Plant Availability Factor (PAF) fell to 74.75% from 78.87% — a genuine operating miss, driven overwhelmingly by Teesta-V (510 MW) contributing zero generation for the entire fiscal year. Management's own reconciliation: ex-Teesta-V, PAF was ~80% in both years (essentially flat, close to the normative 80% standalone / 82% consolidated norm); including Teesta-V it was 75% — i.e., one non-generating asset alone cost the group roughly 4 points of consolidated availability.

Two flagship new assets are being billed at 75–80% of filed tariff — ₹450 Cr of FY26 revenue is not yet booked

⚠
Parbati-II and Subansiri Lower are running well below their design-energy economics, and it isn't just a billing lagParbati-II (800 MW, commissioned FY26) generated only 1,642 million units against a 3,125 MU design energy in FY26 — roughly half — largely traced to a June 2025 cloudburst event, and posted a full-year loss of ₹150 crore despite a Q4-alone profit of ₹104 crore (the Q4 profit itself driven by a one-off ₹200 Cr generation-shortfall recovery booked under revenue, not underlying improvement). Subansiri Lower similarly posted a full-year profit of ₹495 Cr but management quantified a further ₹150 Cr gross under-recovery there. Combined, management stated ₹450 crore of revenue "has not been booked in our book of accounts" against the tariff petitions actually filed with CERC for these two assets, pending interim/final tariff orders (Parbati-II hearing expected to conclude by end-June 2026 per the May call; Subansiri Lower's timeline runs several months behind that). Billing is currently capped at 75% of filed AFC for Parbati-II and revenue is being recognised at a conservative 80% of AFC for both projects — meaning even the reported revenue embeds a management assumption, not a locked-in number.

This under-recovery is a genuine, quantified near-term catalyst if and when CERC issues final orders (management says recognition would flow through with no deferral once an order lands, including interest from COD) — but it is also evidence that two of the group's largest, newest, highest-capex assets are not yet earning close to their intended regulated return, more than a year after Parbati-II's commissioning.

Segment, geography and expansion

No formal segment reporting exists beyond "electricity generation" as the sole reportable segment; trading/consultancy/contracts revenue is immaterial and undisclosed separately. Geographically, generation is entirely domestic (India), though NHPC's business description also references Nepal exposure (MarketData business summary) not separately quantified in the financial statements reviewed. Vertical/horizontal expansion is clear and substantial: from pure hydro into utility-scale solar (Bikaner 300 MW commissioned; further ~1,190 MW across Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, Odisha and Kerala floating solar in the pipeline) and into pumped-storage (18 GW of PSP capacity at DPR/PFR stage across seven states, per the call) — a genuine diversification beyond conventional run-of-river hydro, though none of it yet contributes materially to reported revenue.

⚠
Reported FY26 PAT growth of 25% overstates the underlying trend by management's own mathOn the Q4FY26 call, when asked for an "Adjusted PAT," CFO Saroj Kumar Roy explained that NHPC's move to the 25% flat-tax regime under Section 115BAA (ahead of MAT-credit discontinuation from FY27) required restating previously-created deferred tax liabilities, producing a one-off gross benefit of ₹1,156 crore (₹900 crore net) recognised in Q4FY26 — management's own words: "if you reduce Rs. 900 Crore from the Reported PAT, you will arrive at Adjusted PAT." Applying that adjustment to the FY26 full-year owners' PAT of ₹3,766 Cr implies an adjusted figure of roughly ₹2,866 Cr — below FY25's reported ₹3,007 Cr, meaning core, non-one-off profitability likely contracted year-on-year even as the headline number grew 25%. The tax-regime shift is a genuine, recurring positive for future years' effective tax rate (see §08), but conflating this one-off restatement with organic earnings growth would materially overstate FY26's quality.

What actually moved the P&L in FY26 (company's own reconciliation)

Per the Q4FY26 call and financial-results notes, FY26 vs FY25 (full year, consolidated): revenue up ₹1,235 Cr on new-capacity generation; other income down ₹164 Cr (a large prior-year insurance business-interruption claim on Teesta-V did not repeat); generation expenses up a modest ₹22 Cr (water cess); employee costs down ₹325 Cr (a one-off pay-anomaly reversal); finance costs up ₹234 Cr as Parbati-II/Subansiri/Karnisar borrowing costs began hitting the P&L post-COD; depreciation up ₹783 Cr for the same reason; and, the largest single swing, other expenses up ₹1,938 Cr (₹2,122 Cr → ₹4,060 Cr) — driven by higher network access charges (+₹822 Cr), insurance expense (+₹476 Cr), insurance-claim-related losses (+₹225 Cr), provisions (+₹168 Cr) and R&M expense (+₹111 Cr). That last item is the real explanation for the EBITDA-margin compression in the table above: revenue grew, but non-generation operating costs grew far faster, eating most of the operating leverage a capacity ramp should otherwise deliver.

Forward margin guidance

No explicit numeric margin target was given on the call. The clearest forward signal is qualitative and structural: management confirmed the FY26 one-off tax benefit reflects a permanent shift to a lower (25%) statutory rate from FY27, which should support the effective tax rate (and thus net margin, all else equal) going forward even without repeating the one-off DTL credit — a genuine, if modest, tailwind distinct from the one-off itself.

Against a disclosed project pipeline whose costs sum to roughly ₹1.3 lakh crore (§01), a balance sheet growing debt faster than equity every year is the central financing risk in this story.

Capital allocation: dividend cut despite higher reported profit

FY26 total dividend was ₹1.61/share (₹1.40 interim + ₹0.21 final, 16.10% of ₹10 face value) versus ₹1.91/share for FY25 (₹1.40 interim + ₹0.51 final) — a roughly 16% cut in per-share cash return in the same year reported PAT grew 25%. Share count is unchanged (1,004.5 crore shares throughout), so this is a genuine capital-allocation choice, not a per-share dilution effect: NHPC is retaining more of a (partly one-off-inflated, §04) profit to help fund the capex programme rather than passing the reported earnings growth through to shareholders. Given the scale of the pipeline in §01, that is a defensible choice for a growth-stage regulated utility, but it is worth being explicit that dividend growth has not tracked reported earnings growth.

Trade receivables & the regulatory-deferral mechanic

Reported trade receivables were ₹2,630 Cr at 31 Mar 2026 (vs ₹2,573 Cr a year earlier), but ₹1,887 Cr of that is unbilled debtors (revenue recognised, not yet billed — consistent with the 80%-of-AFC recognition practice in §03), leaving genuinely billed-and-outstanding receivables of just ₹743 Cr, of which ₹242 Cr is more than 45 days overdue. Realisation from beneficiaries against energy sales was ₹10,499 Cr in FY26 vs ₹9,943 Cr in FY25 — i.e., cash collections did grow, just not as fast as booked revenue, consistent with the CFO gap documented in §06.

For a utility mid-way through a multi-year capacity build this is not unusual in principle — but it does mean the entire growth pipeline in §01 is currently being financed externally, and the balance sheet has to keep absorbing debt at a faster pace than operating cash flow is growing, which per §04's finding is itself decelerating on a core basis.

Other call insights worth flagging

Management's own framing of cost-overrun risk, the REIA tendering pipeline, and a tax-regime shift that outlasts the one-off it created.

"Cost overrun is very inherent in any hydropower project" — management's own words

Asked directly whether other under-construction projects might see cost escalations similar to Pakal Dul/Kiru, CFO Saroj Kumar Roy answered candidly rather than defensively: "Cost escalation in hydro projects are very inherent. You cannot rule out. Even though if there is no time overrun, there will be cost overrun... it is not necessary that every project will have their time overrun... [but] cost overrun is very inherent in any hydropower project." He characterised current escalations across the pipeline as "not material," but the admission itself — volunteered, not extracted — is a useful baseline expectation for modelling this pipeline: assume some cost drift is structural, not a one-off surprise, and remember the CERC cost-plus framework passes most of it through to the regulated equity base (§01) rather than eating into margins directly, provided the escalation is judged beyond management's control.

REIA tendering: a large backlog, only partly converted

As Renewable Energy Implementing Agency, NHPC has awarded roughly 23 GW of renewable capacity to date; management said ~13 GW of PPA/PSA signing is "likely," of which ~7 GW had actually been signed as of the May call — i.e., a genuine multi-GW backlog still awaiting contractual close, separate from NHPC's own generation pipeline. No tendering had occurred "in recent past" as of the call, suggesting the REIA pipeline itself was not being actively replenished at that point.

Weather/monsoon risk explicitly downplayed

Asked about El Niño-related weaker-monsoon risk, management's response leaned on the asset mix rather than dismissing the risk outright: "most of our power stations are snow-fed... certainly good monsoon will be helping us for better generation but our generation currently also is going fairly well" — a reasonable point (snow-fed hydrology is less monsoon-dependent than run-of-river schemes on purely rain-fed rivers) but not a guarantee, and PAF has already missed its normative level in FY26 for reasons unrelated to monsoon (§03).

The tax-regime shift is structural, not one-off

Beyond the Q4FY26 one-off DTL adjustment flagged in §04, NHPC confirmed (Q1FY27 results notes) it has elected into the Section 200(5) option under the Income Tax Act 2025 from FY27 onward — the same lower-rate regime that produced the FY26 restatement. This should mean a structurally lower effective tax rate on ongoing operations from FY27, a genuine (if modest, and already partly reflected in consensus forward estimates — §10) tailwind distinct from the one-off credit itself.

Bottom line

What's good: a genuinely large, government-backed, regulated-return capacity build-out with disclosed project costs summing to roughly ₹1.3 lakh crore, most of it already substantially progressed (Pakal Dul and Kiru both 80%+ complete, Rangit-IV 96%); a CERC cost-plus tariff framework that passes most uncontrollable cost escalation through to the regulated equity base rather than eating margins; regulated equity guided to grow 67% within a year (₹18,309 Cr → ₹30,672 Cr); a structurally lower 25% tax rate from FY27; management that answers hard questions directly on calls (the candid "cost overrun is very inherent" admission, the volunteered Adjusted-PAT walk-through); a clean, unmodified audit opinion; low beta (0.19) and a comparatively narrow trading range consistent with a defensive utility profile; and a large, still-growing renewable/PSP optionality pipeline (18 GW PSP at DPR/PFR stage, ~13 GW REIA PPA/PSA pipeline).

What's not good: FY26's headline 25% PAT growth was substantially a one-off deferred-tax restatement by management's own admission — adjusted PAT likely declined versus FY25; operating cash flow fell 34% and broke a three-year run of clean CFO/PAT conversion in the same year; free cash flow has been negative in three of the last four years and increasingly so (−₹8,279 Cr in FY26); "Other Expenses" more than tripled since FY24, eating most of the operating leverage a capacity ramp should deliver; PAF fell to 74.75% largely because a 510 MW asset (Teesta-V) generated zero revenue for the entire fiscal year; ₹450 crore of revenue from the two newest, largest commissioned assets remains unbooked with tariff orders already running behind management's own guided timeline; debt is up 26% YoY funding a pipeline several multiples the size of current net worth, with interest coverage already falling (5.89x → 3.61x YoY); the dividend was cut despite higher reported profit; a fatal tunnel explosion (10 dead) just hit an active project site central to the growth thesis; and the government sold up to 6% of its own stake the same quarter, an overhang independent of operating results.

How management has navigated it: competently and candidly on execution detail — the project-by-project cost/progress/timeline disclosure on the call is unusually granular for a PSU, and the Adjusted-PAT explanation was volunteered rather than extracted under pressure. Less reassuringly on framing: the "12% revenue growth, 25% PAT growth" headlines in the opening remarks were delivered without foregrounding the one-off tax item's scale, and several specific near-term commitments given on the May call (Parbati-II tariff order "by end of this quarter," AP solar "by June") had not visibly landed as of this report's date. No Q1FY27 call transcript exists in this dataset to check whether management addressed the Teesta-VI incident, the OFS, or the tariff-order slippage directly with analysts.

AI-Synthesized ViewLikelihood the next 12 months resolve in the bulls' favour from today's starting price: moderate, roughly 40–50%. The capacity-build thesis is real and substantially de-risked on an execution basis (most flagship projects are 70–96% physically complete), and the regulated-tariff framework means growing installed capacity should eventually show up in earnings almost mechanically. But this report's own tabulated numbers show FY26's reported growth leaned more on a tax one-off than on operating momentum, cash conversion deteriorated in the same year, and several of management's own near-term commitments have already run behind schedule — a pattern investors should expect to continue given the company's own acknowledgment that cost and time overrun are "very inherent" to hydropower construction. The market's forward P/E of 12.4x already prices a large EPS step-up; the stock's risk/reward from here depends less on whether the capacity gets built (it substantially will) than on whether the CERC tariff orders and execution timelines land inside FY27 as guided, or slip again as they already have once.
