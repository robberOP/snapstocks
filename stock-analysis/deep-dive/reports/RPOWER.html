Equity Research Note · Power Generation — Coal, Gas & Renewables (Independent Power Producer)

Reliance Power Limited

NSE: RPOWER · BSE: 532939 · FY2023–FY2027(Q1) performance review, BSE-filing and press-release synthesis, as of 23 August 2026.

The one-line story: Reliance Power has spent the past two years quietly deleveraging — consolidated debt down 30% from ₹21,236 Cr (FY23) to ₹14,812 Cr (FY26), finance costs down 19% YoY in FY26, and operating EBITDA margins holding a fairly stable 27–32% band across seven straight quarters — but that slow, genuine progress has been swamped by an escalating cluster of legal and governance shocks. On 29 April 2026, lender Export-Import Bank of the United States filed a Section 7 insolvency application against the listed parent company itself (not merely a subsidiary), over a $165 million guaranteed default at gas-based subsidiary Samalkot Power; two subsidiaries (Samalkot, Rajasthan Sun Technique Energy) carry a combined negative net worth exceeding ₹5,400 crore and are in active default; the FY26 audit opinion is qualified on going-concern grounds; a SEBI forensic audit, an Enforcement Directorate PMLA investigation with over ₹1,000 crore in provisional asset attachments, a prosecution complaint that saw a former Executive Director & CFO arrested, and a CBI search-and-seizure at the registered office are all live and unresolved as this report is written. Meanwhile over ₹300 crore of investor warrants lapsed unconverted even as the board sought shareholder authorization for a fresh ₹9,000 crore capital raise. The stock trades at 0.6x book value and has lost more than half its value from its 52-week high.

01

What Reliance Power does, and how it has grown

A 5,305 MW power-generation holding company built around one giant, captive-coal-backed asset — now trying to add renewables and an unfunded "AI" pivot on top of a balance sheet still working through two insolvent subsidiaries.

Incorporated in 1995 (CIN L40101MH1995PLC084687) and headquartered in Mumbai, Reliance Power Limited is part of the Reliance Group (the Anil Ambani–led ADAG group, distinct from Reliance Industries). It develops, owns and operates power-generation assets across coal, gas, hydro, wind and solar (MarketData/overview.md business summary), and describes itself in its own FY26 annual report as "a leading private sector power generation company in India" with total installed capacity of 5,305 MW. The company employs 1,278 people directly (overview.md) and held its 32nd Annual General Meeting on 14 August 2026.

The asset base

The flagship asset is the Sasan Ultra Mega Power Project in Madhya Pradesh — 3,960 MW across six 660 MW coal-fired units, described in the FY26 MD&A as "one of the largest integrated coal-based power plants globally," backed by captive coal mines that the company cites as giving it fuel security and cost competitiveness most peers lack post-2015 coal-block-auction reforms. The Rosa Power Project in Uttar Pradesh (1,200 MW, four 300 MW units) is the second major thermal asset. The Samalkot Power Project (Andhra Pradesh, gas-based, owned via subsidiary Samalkot Power Limited/SMPL) has been non-operational for years on gas-availability and commercial-viability grounds; the company now describes a "planned exit" from the asset (§05, §08) rather than a turnaround plan. Renewables today are small in absolute terms: a 40 MW Dhursar solar-PV plant, a 100 MW Concentrated Solar Power (CSP) thermal plant (one of India's earliest such installations) and a 5 MW wind project in Tamil Nadu.

The renewables pipeline — large on paper, unfunded so far

Per the FY26 annual report, the company has "secured" three renewable-plus-storage projects: 930 MW solar + 465 MW/1,860 MWh BESS; 350 MW solar + 175 MW/700 MWh BESS; and 650 MW solar + 750 MW/3,300 MWh BESS — roughly 1,930 MW of solar and 1,390 MW/5,860 MWh of battery storage in aggregate. Separately, an associate company, Reliance Enterprises Private Limited, has announced a 50:50 joint venture with Bhutan's Druk Holdings and Investment to develop a 500 MW solar project and a 770 MW hydroelectric project in Bhutan — but the company's own auditor note describes this associate as "only incorporated, no investment has been made as on date," so this is an announcement, not a funded commitment. §05 shows why this pipeline should be read cautiously: FY26 net capex was actually negative (asset base shrinking, not growing).

The newest pivot: renaming four subsidiaries toward "AI"

On 30 June 2026 the company disclosed that, "as a step to participate in the rapidly evolving field of Artificial Intelligence," four existing subsidiaries were renamed — Reliance AI Green Power Private Limited, Reliance AI Power Private Limited, Reliance AI Data Control Private Limited and Reliance AI Data C Private Limited — and their objects amended to cover "Artificial Intelligence and technology-enabled services." The filing itself calls these "enabling steps": no capex commitment, land, customer, or partner was disclosed alongside the renaming. This report treats the AI/data-centre angle as aspirational-stage only, not an evidenced business line, until a specific project, contract or capex line appears in a subsequent filing.

The moat, such as it's evidenced

What is independently verifiable: captive coal linkage at Sasan (a genuinely scarce, hard-to-replicate advantage under India's current coal-allocation regime); long-term Power Purchase Agreements across the operating thermal fleet, which the company's own SWOT disclosure cites as giving "clear revenue and cash flow visibility"; and scale (5,305 MW, anchored by one of the world's largest single integrated coal complexes). Against that, the company's own FY26 MD&A SWOT analysis discloses real weaknesses in its own words: "high dependence on thermal power within the active revenue-generating portfolio," "relatively limited operational renewable capacity compared to the massive upcoming project pipeline," and "legacy assets with varying operational and thermal efficiency levels across plants." No independent market-share, ranking or competitor-benchmark figure was found in any reviewed filing — every scale claim in this section is the company's own.

What actually moved margins

Unlike the PAT line, EBITDA margin has been comparatively well-behaved: 26.6% (Q3FY25) to a peak of 32.3% (Q3FY26) back down to 28.8% (Q1FY27) — a real but modest ~5.7-point range across seven quarters, not the kind of swing that would suggest operational deterioration. Finance cost fell from ₹2,055.86 Cr (FY25) to ₹1,666.21 Cr (FY26), −19.0% YoY, directly tracking the debt paydown in §05 — genuine progress. What has not improved: other income and exceptional items remain large relative to the core P&L (other income alone was ₹368.81 Cr in FY26, on top of the exceptional impairment), so headline PAT will likely keep moving in ways the underlying generation business doesn't fully explain until these below-the-line items normalise.

A data-quality caveat on multi-year context

MarketData/yfinance's own annual "EBITDA" and "Total Revenue" figures diverge materially from the BSE-filed figures used above — e.g. yfinance shows FY25 EBITDA of ₹5,844.77 Cr on revenue of ₹7,153.21 Cr (an 81.7% margin, clearly distorted by folding the one-off deconsolidation gain into its own EBITDA definition) versus this report's BSE-derived FY25 EBITDA of ₹2,108.25 Cr (27.8% margin). This report uses the BSE-sourced, internally-cross-checked figures throughout §03–04 rather than yfinance's figures, and flags this discrepancy explicitly rather than silently picking one source.

The insolvency filing against the parent, in detail

On 29 April 2026, Export-Import Bank of the United States (US Exim) filed an application under Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 against Reliance Power Limited itself — the listed parent, not a subsidiary — alleging default of a net debt of $165.41 million by Samalkot Power Limited (SMPL), which the parent had guaranteed. The company's stated defense: the debt "has not fallen due," and SMPL had already filed for arbitration before the London Court of International Arbitration (LCIA) against US Exim and facility agent Citibank N.A. on 29 June 2025, on the ground that no default exists. That defense weakened materially during this report's data window: on 23 June 2026, the LCIA tribunal issued a partial final award holding it did not have jurisdiction over SMPL's claim (without ruling on the substantive merits either way); on 30 July 2026, SMPL withdrew the arbitration. As of the 6 August 2026 financial-results filing — the most recent primary source in this dataset — "the management is currently evaluating the appropriate legal course of action." No NCLT admission, dismissal, or hearing date is disclosed anywhere in the reviewed filings; this is a live, unresolved proceeding as this report is written.

Management's stated resolution path runs through asset monetization rather than litigation alone: on 6 June 2026, SMPL signed an Equipment Sale Agreement with AM Green Energies B.V. for the sale of Module 2 and Module 3 equipment of the 1,508 MW (2×754 MW) combined-cycle plant, "subject to the lender's approval." Management states that "upon consummation of the proposed asset monetization transaction...the outstanding lender obligations to be completely addressed" — a specific, testable claim, still pending as of the last filing reviewed.

Bottom line

What's good: real, measurable multi-year deleveraging — consolidated debt down 30% since FY23, debt/equity nearly halved to 0.92x, finance costs down 19% YoY in FY26; a fairly stable operating business with EBITDA margins holding a 27–32% band across seven straight quarters, anchored by one of the world's largest integrated coal complexes (Sasan) with genuine captive-fuel security; cash from operations that stayed firmly positive in FY26 despite a reported net loss, cleanly explained by a non-cash impairment rather than a cash-flow problem; credible new operational leadership (Sudhakar Tandon, Abhimanyu Das) with real sector pedigree, plus a finance-credentialed new Independent Director; and a genuinely broad public float (~70%) rather than the concentrated promoter control common at distressed small-caps.

What's not good: an active Section 7 insolvency application against the listed parent itself, filed 29 April 2026, with the company's own legal defense (the LCIA arbitration) having lost on jurisdictional grounds during this report's own data window, and no resolution disclosed as of the last filing reviewed; two subsidiaries (SMPL, RSTEPL) carrying a combined negative net worth exceeding ₹5,400 crore and in active default; a qualified FY26 audit opinion citing going-concern doubt at three subsidiaries; consolidated interest coverage below 1x in FY25 and barely above 1x in FY26; capex running at roughly a tenth of depreciation despite an announced 1,930 MW renewables pipeline and a ₹9,000 crore fundraising authorization, alongside over ₹300 crore of investor warrants that lapsed unconverted in the same window; a simultaneous SEBI forensic audit, an ED PMLA investigation with over ₹1,000 crore in provisional asset attachments, a former Executive Director & CFO arrested in an ED/EOW prosecution complaint, and a CBI search-and-seizure at the registered office — all open at once; an auditor rotation landing the same day as the qualified opinion; and zero analyst coverage to independently stress-test any of management's monetization or resolution claims.

How management has navigated it: operationally, with real if modest progress — debt reduction, targeted operational hires, and a specific (if unclosed) asset-monetization path for the SMPL default. Publicly, every filing note in this dataset leans on a consistent "no impact on the business operations" framing for each new investigation, without an earnings call or investor presentation anywhere to field a direct analyst question on any of it. The pattern across the last three quarters is a company processing a genuinely serious cluster of legal and governance risk almost entirely through compliance-driven BSE disclosure language, which is the minimum required, not the maximum an investor might want given the stakes.

AI-Synthesized ViewLikelihood the next 12 months resolve in the bulls' favour from today's starting price: low-to-moderate, roughly 25–35%. The operating business tabulated in §03–04 and §06 is genuinely more stable than the newsflow alone would suggest, and the deleveraging trend in §05 is real. But the single dominant variable — whether NCLT admits the parent-level CIRP application — is unresolved, binary, and outside this dataset's ability to forecast, and it sits on top of a stack of simultaneously open, separately material regulatory investigations with no visible timeline to resolution and no analyst community independently pressure-testing management's claims. That combination argues for treating the current 0.6x-book valuation as a reasonable starting discount for real risk, not a bargain.
