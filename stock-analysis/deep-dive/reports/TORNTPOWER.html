Equity Research Note · Integrated Power Utility — Generation, Transmission & Distribution

Torrent Power Limited

NSE: TORNTPOWER · BSE: 532779 · FY2022–FY2027(Q1) performance review, BSE-filing and press-release synthesis, as of 23 August 2026.

The one-line story: Torrent Power is mid-way through the largest capex programme in its history — capex nearly doubled to ₹8,681 Cr in FY26, the renewable pipeline target was raised from ~5.95 GWp to ~6.25 GWp between the May and August 2026 investor decks, and the company closed a ₹3,632 Cr acquisition of the 1,400 MW Nabha coal plant on 25 June 2026 — while its regulated Distribution business kept doing exactly what it's known for (loss of 2.33%, ranked #1 nationally among 65 DISCOMs, EBITDA +10% YoY in Q1FY27). The bill for that expansion is arriving in real time: free cash flow turned negative (−₹2,240 Cr) in FY26 for the first time in the visible record, finance costs jumped 38% YoY in Q1FY27 as debt funded the Nabha deal and a fresh ₹10,000 Cr NCD programme, net debt/equity has roughly doubled toward ~0.97x, and return ratios slid from FY25's flattered highs (RONW 18.4%, aided by a one-off ₹637 Cr deferred-tax reversal and a ₹3,440 Cr equity raise) to a more pedestrian 12.2% in FY26. The thermal/gas Generation segment, exposed to LNG price and supply swings, actually posted a segment loss in Q4FY26. The stock has fallen roughly 12% since Q1 results day to ₹1,247.70, near its 52-week low and below both its 50- and 200-day averages, even as both CRISIL and India Ratings affirm AA+/Stable and a well-credentialed external CFO (ex-Indus Towers) takes over from a retiring one.

01

What Torrent Power does, and how it has grown

A rare Indian private-sector play spanning the entire power value chain — generation, transmission and distribution — now in the middle of doubling its generation footprint.

Incorporated in 2004 and based in Ahmedabad, Torrent Power is part of the Torrent Group (which also owns listed Torrent Pharmaceuticals and Torrent Gas, the latter filing draft IPO papers for up to $418m in August 2026 — News, §02). The company sits across three operating segments per its own Ind AS 108 disclosure: Generation (thermal — gas and coal — plus LNG regasification/trading), Transmission & Distribution (licensed distribution in Ahmedabad, Gandhinagar, Surat, Dahej SEZ, Dholera SIR and the UT of Dadra & Nagar Haveli/Daman & Diu, plus franchised distribution in Bhiwandi, Agra and Shil-Mumbra-Kalwa, plus the transmission subsidiary TPGL), and Renewables (wind and solar). As of 30 June 2026 the company had 6.6 GWp of operational generation capacity (2.7 GW gas, 1.8 GW coal including the newly acquired Nabha plant, 2.1 GWp renewables) plus 388 ckm of 220kV and 952 ckm of 400kV transmission lines, and distributed ~31 billion units annually to 4.29 million customers (Investor Presentation, Q1 FY27).

FY22's TCI is depressed by a ₹928 Cr net-of-tax impairment provision (yfinance shows a ₹1,300 Cr gross impairment that year) — not a core-earnings collapse.

Revenue nearly doubled from FY22 to FY23 as the low base normalised post-impairment, then grew more modestly through FY25 before FY26 revenue actually declined fractionally (−0.7%) even as EBITDA rose 1.2% — the mix shift (below) explains why. FY25's headline RONW of 18.4% flatters the trend: it was aided by a one-off ₹637 Cr non-cash deferred-tax-liability reversal and a ₹3,440 Cr equity raise that lifted net worth 42.7% in a single year (yfinance balance-sheet series). Strip those two effects out and FY26's 12.2% RONW — the lowest print in this five-year window — reads less like a bad year in isolation and more like a return to a lower, capex-diluted baseline as the current expansion programme (§05) weighs on returns before new assets start earning.

The moat: a regulated distribution franchise that is genuinely best-in-class

The clearest, most independently evidenced competitive advantage is Distribution, not Generation. Licensed distribution loss stood at 2.33% in FY26 — the company states this ranks #1 nationally among 65 DISCOMs — and its Ahmedabad and Surat circles received an A+ grade, ranking first out of 54 utilities nationwide in the 14th Integrated Ratings for FY25 (individual score of 100.00, per the April 2026 India Ratings credit note). The Bhiwandi franchisee — the country's first private distribution-franchisee arrangement of its kind — has cut AT&C losses from 58.77% at takeover to ~5.4% in FY26; Agra similarly from 58% to ~9.1%. Power availability across licensed areas is 99.9%. These are hard-to-replicate, multi-decade operational track records under regulated cost-plus/ROE tariff structures (CERC-regulated transmission earns a post-tax ROE of 15–15.5% plus incentives per the Investor Presentation) — a genuinely defensible base that the current Generation/Renewables expansion is being built on top of, not instead of.

Segment mix: revenue is concentrated in Distribution, profit less so

FY26 consolidated segment revenue was 74% Transmission & Distribution, 22% Generation and just 4% Renewables (before inter-segment elimination, on a ₹33,591 Cr gross-segment-revenue base) — but segment EBITDA (segment result) was a less lopsided 62% T&D, 22% Generation and 16% Renewables, meaning the small Renewables segment already punches well above its revenue weight in profit terms, while Generation's profit share roughly tracks its revenue share. This mix is the backdrop for §03's finding that the Generation segment's profitability is also the most volatile of the three by a wide margin.

What moved FY26 and Q1FY27

FY26 EBITDA grew a modest 1.2% on flat-to-declining revenue, so the EBITDA margin held around 20% — a reasonably stable print. Below EBITDA, D&A rose 7.7% (₹1,497 Cr → ₹1,613 Cr) as new renewable and thermal assets under construction started depreciating, consistent with the capex-vs-depreciation cross-check in §05. PAT margin's apparent FY25→FY26 decline (10.4%→8.4%) is mostly a base-effect artefact of FY25's abnormally low tax charge rather than a genuine deterioration in operating economics — but the Q4FY26 segment-level Generation loss (§03) and the Q1FY27 finance-cost jump are real, current-period pressures layered on top of that base effect, not just an accounting comparison problem.

Quarterly margin volatility, driven almost entirely by Generation

Consolidated EBITDA margin has actually been fairly stable quarter to quarter (19.0%–21.7% across the six quarters in §03) — markedly less volatile than the segment-level swings in §03 would suggest, because T&D's steady ~62% share of segment profit smooths the group number even as Generation whipsaws underneath it. This is a genuine structural strength: an investor looking only at the consolidated margin line would miss the Q4FY26 Generation-segment loss entirely.

Forward margin guidance

None found in numeric form (see §03). Management's own framing for FY26 in the results press release cited “improved operational performance of licensed and franchised distribution businesses,” renewable growth “partly offset by higher interest and depreciation cost as the growth continues,” and gas-based generation “remaining constrained” — directionally consistent with everything tabulated above, and about as close to explicit forward margin guidance as this dataset gets.

Credit ratings: unaffected so far, both AA+/Stable

Both CRISIL Ratings (23 Apr 2026) and India Ratings (16 Apr 2026) affirmed Torrent Power's long-term rating at AA+/Stable and short-term at A1+, even while assigning fresh ratings to new NCD issuance (₹4,000 Cr from CRISIL; ₹3,700 Cr from India Ratings) — both actions predate the Nabha close and the full NCD draw-down, so they should be read as a snapshot of credit quality going into this leverage cycle, not a verdict on it. India Ratings' own note explicitly flags gas-based plants operating at “lower [PLF] in the first nine months of fiscal 2026, as power demand grew at a slower pace,” corroborating the Generation-segment weakness independently documented in §03–04 from a source outside the company's own filings.

Contingent liabilities & related-party notes

Standalone disputed claims (income tax, sales/service tax, customs, stamp duty, VAT, GST, other) totalled ₹463.9 Cr as at 31 March 2026, up from ~₹267 Cr a year earlier — the increase is almost entirely a new ₹214.4 Cr disputed GST demand (of which ₹189.4 Cr relates to a March 2026 demand order on rent for electricity distribution transformers in Agra, which the company is contesting and for which the relevant UP government authority has itself indicated the rent should not apply). At ~1% of the FY26 balance sheet this is not currently material, but it is a genuine new item worth tracking rather than a housekeeping footnote. No auditor qualification, emphasis of matter beyond standard Key Audit Matters, or change of statutory auditor was found in the Annual Report or the board outcome letter (which explicitly states the FY26 auditor's report “contains unmodified opinion”). This report did not exhaustively review the Annual Report's full related-party-transaction note given its length (33,600+ lines); no unusual or outsized related-party item surfaced in the sections searched, but that is a targeted-search finding, not an exhaustive one, and should be read as such.

Read plainly: FY26 is the first year in this five-year window where free cash flow turned negative, and the company financed the gap externally — CFF swung from −₹1,216 Cr (FY25, still funding growth mostly from the prior year's equity raise and internal generation) to +₹3,101 Cr (FY26, net new debt issuance of ₹5,016 Cr). Management's own capex guidance (§03: ₹10,000 Cr for FY27 renewables alone) implies FCF stays negative for at least another year, and likely longer once the Anuppur thermal project (>₹30,000 Cr committed) and the 3 GW pumped-storage project (₹14,000 Cr) draw down in earnest. No management commentary on a target date for FCF turning positive, or a specific multi-year capex-funding plan beyond the disclosed ₹35,000 Cr overall borrowing-limit headroom, was found in this dataset.

Sabarmati retirement: a genuinely open-ended replacement plan

The 3 Aug 2026 announcement that the 362 MW Sabarmati coal plant will retire by December 2030 is notable less for the retirement itself (a small, old plant, and only ~6% of current coal capacity even after Nabha) than for how unresolved the replacement is: the planned 800 MW unit's location is explicitly “work in progress,” central-government coal allocation approval is still pending, and land negotiations for the ash pond with the Gujarat government are ongoing. This is a multi-year, multi-contingency project still at an early planning stage — a useful reminder that not every capacity number in §03's pipeline table carries the same execution certainty as, say, the Anuppur project (PPA signed, land in place, EPC awarded).

Distribution-sector policy tailwind, unquantified

Bottom line

What's good: a genuinely best-in-class regulated Distribution franchise (2.33% licensed distribution loss, #1 nationally among 65 DISCOMs, A+/#1 Integrated Rating for Ahmedabad and Surat, Bhiwandi AT&C losses cut from 58.77% to 5.4%) that keeps compounding steadily (segment EBITDA +10% YoY in Q1FY27) regardless of what's happening elsewhere in the business; a large, substantially de-risked capacity pipeline with tariffs and PPAs already locked in for much of the renewable and thermal build (Anuppur's PPA signed, land in place, EPC awarded; PSP tied to a 40-year MSEDCL agreement); Nabha adds a contracted, 25-year-PPA coal asset with guided ₹1,000 Cr steady-state EBITDA; both CRISIL and India Ratings affirm AA+/Stable even as leverage rises; a clean, unmodified FY26 audit opinion; healthy underlying cash generation with CFO consistently exceeding profit (no earnings-quality red flag, unlike the FCF-negative headline alone might suggest); and an orderly CFO succession into a well-credentialed external hire with directly relevant capital-intensive-infrastructure experience.

What's not good: the Generation (thermal/gas) segment posted an outright segment loss in Q4FY26 and remains the most volatile piece of the business, exposed to LNG price and supply swings management itself has flagged in two consecutive quarters' press releases; FY26 free cash flow turned negative for the first time in this five-year record as capex nearly doubled, and FY27 guidance (₹10,000 Cr renewables capex alone) implies that continues for at least another year; net debt/equity has roughly doubled in two quarters toward ~0.97x on the debt-funded Nabha acquisition and a fresh ₹10,000 Cr NCD programme, with total group borrowing headroom raised to ₹35,000 Cr; return ratios have fallen from FY25's flattered highs (RONW 18.4%, aided by a one-off tax reversal and an equity raise) to a more ordinary 12.2% in FY26; finance costs jumped 38% YoY in Q1FY27 alone and are now the single biggest swing factor in quarterly PAT; and no readable earnings-call transcript exists anywhere in this dataset, meaning this report could not check management's own real-time framing and defence of any of these numbers in a Q&A setting.

How management has navigated it: consistently and without evasion on the disclosure side — both quarters' press releases name the same headwinds (gas market volatility, financing costs from growth capex) that this report's own segment and cash-flow tables independently confirm, rather than papering over them with a rosier narrative. On execution, the approach has been aggressive and front-loaded: closing a large debt-funded acquisition, raising a large NCD programme, and lifting the renewable capacity target, all within roughly one quarter, while return ratios and free cash flow both moved the wrong way at the same time. That is a coherent, disclosed strategy — not a governance failure — but it is also a strategy that requires the next several quarters of capacity commissioning and Generation-segment stabilisation to go right before the balance-sheet trade-off it has made starts paying for itself.
