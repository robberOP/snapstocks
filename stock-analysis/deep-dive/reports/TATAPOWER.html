The Tata Power Company Limited

NSE: TATAPOWER · BSE: 500400 · Consolidated basis unless stated otherwise, FY2023–FY2027(Q1) review from BSE filings, earnings-call transcripts and market data, as of 23 August 2026.

The one-line story: Tata Power's underlying growth engine — regulated transmission & distribution capex, a solar cell/module manufacturing business that has finally hit a profitability inflection, and a rooftop-solar franchise scaling fast enough that management just pulled its own 2030 revenue target forward to 2029 — is genuinely working. But FY26, the year management calls its best ever ("PAT crossed ₹5,000 crore for the first time"), is also the year operating cash flow halved (₹12,680cr→₹5,993cr) while reported profit rose, capex delivery missed its own guidance by roughly 45%, and profit attributable to Tata Power's own shareholders (as opposed to the group total including minority partners) actually fell — EPS declined from ₹12.41 to ₹11.71. Layer on a ≈$490 million unprovisioned arbitration award now awaiting a Singapore court ruling, a formal Key Audit Matter on going-concern liquidity structure, and net leverage climbing for the fourth straight year, and the "record year" headline needs more asterisks than the press release gives it.

01

What Tata Power does, and how it has grown

India's largest vertically-integrated private power company — generation, transmission, distribution and a genuinely fast-growing "new energy" arm bolted on top — built through 111 years of regulated-utility ownership and, in the last five, an aggressive push into manufacturing and rooftop solar.

Founded in 1915 and headquartered in Mumbai, Tata Power is organised, per its own segment reporting, into four businesses: Thermal & Hydro (coal, gas, oil and hydro generation, including hydro pump storage and Indonesian coal mining/trading/shipping via joint ventures), Transmission & Distribution (Mumbai and Delhi distribution licences, four Odisha discoms acquired via privatisation, Ajmer distribution, plus TBCB-model inter-state transmission lines), Renewables (utility-scale wind and solar generation, rooftop solar, solar cell/module manufacturing, EV charging, and third-party solar EPC — now being wound down, see §04), and Others (project management/infrastructure services, property development, satellite communications). As of 31 March 2026 the company had 16,716 MW of installed capacity; including projects under construction, total portfolio capacity was ≈26.6 GW, of which 17.7 GW (including 9.8 GW under construction) is classified as clean & green.

It is also, as §04 shows in detail, not the same thing as growth in profit attributable to Tata Power's own shareholders — a distinction that matters a great deal for FY26 specifically and that the headline figures above do not surface.

The moat, and where it actually sits

Roughly two-thirds of segment revenue (T&D: ≈60% of FY26 gross segment revenue) sits in regulated or long-term-contracted businesses — cost-plus tariffs with a permitted return on equity for Mumbai/Odisha/Delhi distribution, TBCB (tariff-based competitive bidding) concessions for new transmission lines with 25–35-year terms once commissioned. This is the classic utility moat: predictable, government-sanctioned returns, insulated from merchant power-price swings, funded by investment-grade debt. Layered on top is a genuinely differentiated new-energy franchise: an integrated 4.9 GW solar cell-and-module manufacturing plant (feeding both third-party utility-scale sales and Tata Power's own captive rooftop/utility projects, with DCR/ALMM compliance as a structural tailwind), and a rooftop-solar business management describes as India's #1 for eleven consecutive years, with 12–13% market share against a #2 competitor at just 2%, built on a 3,778-partner channel network. Captive Indonesian coal joint ventures (KPC, BSSR, AGM) partially hedge the imported-coal cost exposure of the Mundra thermal plant, though as §08 discusses, Mundra's current tariff structure caps this at cost-recovery rather than profit.

What is not independently evidenced in this dataset: no market-share or competitive-ranking data was found for the regulated generation/transmission/distribution segments beyond management's own self-reported figures, and no geography breakdown exists outside India and the Bhutan hydro joint ventures (Khorlochhu, Dorjilung). Growth beyond the domestic market is limited to these two Bhutan projects and legacy Indonesian coal-mining stakes.

Two things stand out. First, revenue and total PAT both stepped up meaningfully from Q2/Q3FY26's trough back through Q4FY26 into Q1FY27 — consistent with the "27th consecutive quarter of PAT and EBITDA growth" claim CEO Praveer Sinha made on the July 2026 call (a claim this note's two-call, five-quarter window cannot independently verify in full, but does not contradict either). Revenue reconciliation: two legitimate but different numbers

Q1FY27 revenue from operations, as reported in the audited/reviewed consolidated P&L, was ₹19,051.26 Cr, +5.6% YoY against Q1FY26's ₹18,035.07 Cr. Management's own investor-presentation slides instead lead with a net-of-regulatory-deferral, segment-reconciliation revenue figure — ₹18,881.44 Cr in Q1FY27 versus ₹17,452.54 Cr in Q1FY26, +8.2% YoY — which is the basis for the "+8% YoY" figure quoted in the press release and the Aug-2026 news item above. FY26's "record PAT" is a group-total figure — owners’ EPS fell 5.6%Total consolidated PAT (before splitting out minority interest) rose 7.2% in FY26 to ₹5,117.56 Cr, comfortably supporting management's "PAT crossed ₹5,000 crore for the first time" framing on the May 2026 call. But minority interest — the share of profit going to co-investors in subsidiaries and joint ventures, not to Tata Power's own shareholders — jumped 70.4% YoY (₹804.37 Cr → ₹1,370.37 Cr), taking its share of total group profit from 16.9% to 26.8% in a single year. The residual, profit actually attributable to Tata Power shareholders, fell 5.6% (₹3,971.0 Cr → ₹3,747.19 Cr), and diluted EPS fell in step (₹12.41 → ₹11.71). The §03 quarterly table shows this wasn't a full-year phenomenon: it was concentrated in Q2–Q4FY26 (minority interest's share peaked at 35.4% in Q3FY26) before reverting to a normal ≈16% in Q1FY27. Neither earnings call transcript in this dataset offers a specific explanation for the swing; a plausible driver is minority stakes in fast-growing renewable subsidiaries and/or the Odisha discoms (which posted an 84% PAT jump in FY26 per the May call) claiming a larger absolute rupee share of a larger profit pool, but this note could not verify that attribution from the source data and flags it as an open question rather than a confirmed explanation.

What moved FY26 and Q1FY27

EBITDA margin (on the yfinance revenue base) held up: 23.9% (FY23) → 22.8% (FY24) → 23.1% (FY25) → 26.8% (FY26) — actually expanding on this measure, which sits at odds with the "margin compression" language in the news item flagged in §02. On the company's own SEBI-mandated "Operating Margin" ratio (a stricter definition excluding other income, disclosed quarterly in the results filing), the picture is flatter: 15% in both Q1FY26 and Q1FY27, down from 16% for full-year FY26 — broadly stable, not the sharp compression an external analyst note described. Net profit margin (including exceptional items, same SEBI definition) was 7% in both Q1FY26 and Q1FY27, versus 8% for FY26 overall. Neither of this note's two independently-computed margin measures reproduces the specific 22.9%→21.2% move cited in the Aug-2026 analyst note flagged in §02; that figure could not be reconciled from the filed data available here.

Below the EBITDA line, finance costs and D&A both grew in step with the balance sheet expansion detailed in §05: consolidated interest expense rose from ₹4,362.49 Cr (FY25) to ₹4,938.28 Cr (FY26), and D&A from ₹4,116.86 Cr to ₹4,811.09 Cr. Interest coverage (EBIT/finance cost, company-disclosed Interest Service Coverage Ratio) held roughly flat at 2.37x for FY26 and 2.38x for Q1FY27 — adequate, but not a large cushion for a company mid-way through a multi-year, debt-funded capex programme, and one of the lower coverage ratios among large-cap Indian utilities on a like-for-like basis (this note has no peer dataset to benchmark that claim precisely, so it is offered as a qualitative observation, not a ranked comparison).

Two guidance items that missed, one that's a genuine quality-of-earnings flag

⚠
Tata Projects (associate): guided to return to profit in FY27, instead posted a wider loss in the very first FY27 quarterOn the May 2026 call, management stated legacy loss-making projects (the Dedicated Freight Corridor, a Barmer refinery project) were "in the final stages of closing" and guided "it will be back, and it will start making profit from FY '27." On the July 2026 call, an analyst noted Tata Projects' losses had instead widened in Q1FY27; management's response walked the guidance back by (at least) one quarter — "we are at something like just 10% of the legacy projects... possibly maybe one more quarter will have an impact." Consolidated Q1FY27 PAT growth (+11% at the total level) was explicitly cited by management as "offset by lower income from Tata Projects and higher corporate costs" — i.e., this is a real, quantified (if not rupee-disclosed) drag on the reported quarter, not a rounding item.
⚠
A ≈₹250 Cr non-cash deferred-tax credit inflated the Q4FY26/FY26 PAT printAn analyst on the May 2026 call flagged a ≈₹250 Cr deferred-tax-asset recognition in the standalone Q4FY26 results (management confirmed the standalone Deferred Tax line moved to a ₹192.60 Cr credit for the quarter, versus a charge in the prior quarter), on the basis of improved visibility into future profitability enabling utilisation of carried-forward tax losses. CFO Sanjeev Churiwala confirmed this "of course" flowed through to consolidated profit positively. This is a legitimate accounting entry, not an irregularity, but it is a non-cash, one-off contributor to the very quarter that pushed full-year PAT over the ₹5,000 Cr milestone — worth separating from the underlying operating trend.

On the positive side of the ledger: Delhi discom prior-period regulatory clearances contributed ₹333 Cr to FY25 and ₹783 Cr to FY26 EBITDA/PAT (a real, cash-eventual, but lumpy and hard-to-forecast tailwind — CFO Churiwala: "next year too we will have something... but to quantify at this stage... might be very, very difficult"), and TP Solar's margin inflection (§03) looks structural rather than one-off, driven by yield/efficiency gains and a genuinely diversified customer mix rather than a single large order.

Forward margin guidance

No explicit consolidated EBITDA-margin or PAT-margin target was found in either transcript. The only forward profitability marker is the FY30E aspiration disclosed in the Q1FY27 investor presentation: EBITDA of ≈₹30,000 Cr (≈2x FY26's ₹16,090 Cr) and PAT of ≈₹10,000 Cr (≈2x FY26's ₹5,212 Cr†) on revenue of ≈₹1,00,000 Cr (≈1.6x) — implying EBITDA and PAT growing meaningfully faster than revenue, consistent with continued margin expansion from the manufacturing and rooftop mix-shift, if delivered.

⚠
Kleros arbitration: ≈$490 million (excluding accruing interest and costs), unprovisioned, with a ruling reservedThe Singapore International Arbitration Centre held Tata Power in breach of non-disclosure and good-faith obligations to Kleros Capital Partners Limited, and in July/August 2025 issued a quantum and final award directing payment of USD 490,320,000 plus simple interest at 5.33% p.a. from 30 November 2020, plus SGD 11,341,963.46 in costs with interest from 1 July 2025. Both the standalone and consolidated FY26 auditor's reports (S R B C & CO LLP) carry an Emphasis of Matter on this item; the Company has appealed to the Singapore International Commercial Court, "does not foresee any affirmative payment obligation," and no provision has been recorded — the Q1FY27 filing states hearings are complete and "the order is reserved," meaning a ruling could land at any time. This is a real, binary, headline-risk event that could land with no further quarter's warning.

Other conference-call insights worth flagging

The Mundra "return to full operations" headline needs a return-on-equity caveat; TBCB revenue booked today is mostly construction accounting, not steady-state profit; and coal-mine monetisation remains an openly-discussed, not-yet-committed option.

Mundra is running, but earning no return on equity

Both calls in this dataset spent significant time on Mundra's regulatory status. The plant now operates under a Supplementary Power Purchase Agreement (SPPA) with Gujarat (effective retrospectively from 1 April 2025, executed 23 March 2026) while simultaneously being scheduled under Section 11 of the Electricity Act pending the remaining procurers' approvals (extended to 30 September 2026). Asked directly on the July call how the SPPA tariff works, CEO Sinha was explicit: "presently, the supplementary PPA is on actual cost of coal, and all parameters... will be on actual. So it's more towards cost reimbursement and cost reflective without any margin to be made from the plant." Pressed further on whether fixed costs are covered, he clarified fixed costs are recovered "but it is cost reflective... you will not get a return on equity." This materially tempers the bullish framing in the 27 July 2026 news item ("Mundra's return to full operations") — the plant is running and no longer a cash drag, which is a real improvement, but it is not yet, and under the current SPPA structure will not become, a profit contributor. Management's stated confidence is about running the plant "continuously now up to 2038," not about restoring the pre-2023 margin profile.

TBCB transmission revenue today is construction accounting, not yet operating profit

An analyst on the July call flagged that the disclosed TBCB (tariff-based competitive bidding) transmission-project EBITDA margin looked implausibly low (≈13%) for what should be a high-margin regulated asset class. CFO Churiwala's clarification: "right now, all our TBCB are under construction... the revenues in the PAT are more from a lease accounting perspective rather than the actual sharing of EBITDA." Real operating EBITDA/PAT contribution from these projects (Jalpura-Khurja, Bikaner-Neemrana and others) is expected to start flowing from Q2FY27 as they are commissioned. This is a useful caution for anyone extrapolating current transmission-cluster margins forward: today's reported numbers understate the eventual steady-state economics of the TBCB pipeline, but only once it's actually commissioned.

Coal-mine monetisation: openly discussed, not committed

Asked on the May 2026 call whether the long-discussed plan to monetise Indonesian coal-mine stakes (KPC, BSSR, AGM) was still active now that the Mundra SPPA process was nearing completion, CEO Sinha's answer was noncommittal: "it also depends on what sort of opportunity is there, what sort of valuation we get... it's a little premature." This is the same optionality referenced, without naming it, in the auditor's going-concern KAM discussion of "planned divestments" (§05) — a real, disclosed lever for capital recycling, but one management itself is not yet ready to commit to or size.

Nuclear and other early-stage optionality

Management confirmed active work with three (unnamed) state governments on land, water-allocation and geotechnical studies for small modular reactors (2×220 MW), with a detailed project report targeted in collaboration with NPCIL within roughly six months of the May 2026 call — contingent on the SHANTI Bill's passage to formally enable private nuclear participation. This remains firmly at the feasibility stage; no capex commitment or timeline beyond the DPR exists in either transcript.

Bottom line

What's good: a genuinely diversified, largely regulated-and-contracted business (≈60% of segment revenue from Transmission & Distribution) with real, credible growth optionality on top — a solar manufacturing business that has finally hit a clean margin inflection (PAT +287% YoY in Q1FY27), a rooftop-solar franchise scaling fast enough that management pulled its own target forward a full year, and a multi-year regulated transmission and hydro pipeline (Bhutan, pumped storage, TBCB) that gives real revenue visibility into the early 2030s. Credit ratings have been upgraded across the board over the last three years. Management continuity (no CEO/CFO/auditor changes in this window) and two recent, genuinely relevant independent-director additions (a former Big Four India chairman, joining a board that needs exactly that skillset given the going-concern KAM and Kleros exposure) are real governance positives.

What's not good: the year management calls its best ever is also the year operating cash flow halved (₹12,680 Cr → ₹5,993 Cr) while reported profit rose — a clean earnings-quality red flag by any standard test; profit and EPS attributable to Tata Power's own shareholders actually fell in FY26 even as the group-total PAT figure management leads with hit a record, because minority interest's claim on profit nearly doubled; FY26 capex delivery missed its own guidance by roughly 45%, with no clear public accounting yet of exactly where the shortfall went beyond "phasing"; Tata Projects' promised return to profitability was pushed back within the very first quarter it was supposed to arrive; and net leverage has climbed for four straight periods after a multi-year deleveraging run. Layered on top: a ≈$490 million unprovisioned arbitration award with a ruling that could land any quarter, and a formal going-concern Key Audit Matter reflecting a genuine, structural working-capital deficit.

How management has navigated it: candidly on specific, pointed analyst questions — the May-2026 capex-shortfall exchange and the July-2026 Tata Projects and TBCB-margin exchanges are all direct, non-evasive answers that this note could tabulate and check — but the framing on both calls consistently emphasises the group-total, "27 consecutive quarters" growth narrative over the owners'-attributable and cash-flow metrics that this report finds tell a meaningfully more mixed story for FY26 specifically. Disclosure quality is generally strong (detailed cluster-level breakdowns, explicit guidance numbers that can be checked quarter to quarter) even where the numbers themselves don't always cooperate with the narrative.

AI-Synthesized ViewLikelihood the bull case is closer to right than the bear case over the next 12 months: moderate, roughly 40–50%. The structural growth story — regulated T&D capex, solar manufacturing margin inflection, rooftop scale — is real and this report's own numbers do not contradict it. But FY26 delivered a genuine pattern of good-story/mixed-numbers (record headline PAT alongside falling owners' EPS, halved operating cash flow, and a large capex guidance miss), and two binary risks (Kleros, going-concern liquidity structure) sit outside the normal quarterly narrative entirely. This is a fundamentally sound, well-run utility-plus-growth conglomerate that the current consensus "Buy" rating and 22x forward multiple already give real credit for — the base case above is close to today's price, which is the honest read of a story with strong tailwinds and real, currently underweighted, execution and disclosure-quality risks.
