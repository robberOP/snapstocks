Equity Research Note · Renewable Energy Generation (Solar / Wind / Hybrid / Storage) — Utilities

Adani Green Energy Limited

NSE: ADANIGREEN · BSE: 541450 · FY2025–FY2027(Q1) performance review, BSE-filing, earnings-call and press-release synthesis, as of 22 August 2026.

The one-line story: AGEL keeps proving it can build faster than almost anyone else in renewables — 5.1 GW of greenfield capacity added in FY26 (the largest annual addition outside China, for the second year running), 20.1 GW operational by Q1FY27, and Q1FY27 revenue/EBITDA up 29%/33% YoY. But the numbers immediately beneath that headline are far bumpier: consolidated group PAT was effectively flat in FY26 (₹1,987 Cr vs ₹2,001 Cr) despite 23% EBITDA growth, one of the last five quarters (Q3FY26) printed an outright loss at the parent level, free cash flow has been negative in both FY25 and FY26 as ₹26,000+ Cr/year of capex outruns operating cash, curtailment cost the company an admitted ₹1,300–1,500 Cr of FY26 EBITDA that management still won't promise is "permanently behind" it, and roughly 4 GW of previously open/merchant capacity plus growing battery-storage volume has just been locked into 25-year fixed-price contracts with sister company Adani Energy Solutions — a related-party restructuring that Bernstein, in a maintained Underperform, has explicitly read as transferring shareholder upside away from AGEL. A long-running US DOJ/SEC matter against certain directors (not the Company) is trending toward settlement but was still formally unresolved as of the Q1FY27 filing. The stock trades at 126x trailing earnings with a Street split between an ₹980 bear case and an ₹1,800 bull case.

01

What AGEL does, and how it has grown

India's largest renewable-energy generator by operating capacity — a pure-play solar/wind/hybrid IPP now adding battery storage and pumped hydro, built through a web of roughly 280 project-level subsidiaries under one listed holding company.

Adani Green Energy Limited (AGEL, CIN L40106GJ2015PLC082007) develops, owns and operates utility-scale, grid-connected renewable generation and storage assets across 12 Indian states — solar (14.2 GW), wind (2.7 GW) and solar-wind hybrid (3.3 GW) plants, plus a fast-growing battery energy storage (BESS) fleet and an early-stage pumped hydro storage (PSP) programme. As of Q1FY27 (30 June 2026) operational capacity stood at 20.1 GW, up 27% YoY, alongside 3.55 GWh of installed BESS capacity. The listed entity itself (standalone) is essentially a holding/EPC/equipment-trading company that reports "only one reportable business segment" under Ind AS-108; the actual generation assets sit inside dozens of wholly-owned and step-down subsidiary SPVs (the Q1FY27 consolidated financial results list 39 audited-by-joint-auditor subsidiaries, 41 audited-by-other-auditor subsidiaries, 102 unaudited/immaterial subsidiaries, and 1 joint venture plus 2 associate companies — a genuinely sprawling structure, disclosed in full in each quarterly filing's Annexure).

Read carefully, FY26 was two different stories stacked on top of each other. Operationally it was an unambiguous record year: the largest greenfield capacity addition outside China for the second consecutive year, 34% energy-sales growth, and a "power supply" EBITDA line up 23%. But at the consolidated group level, reported PAT was essentially flat (−0.7%) — because finance costs, depreciation and minority-interest movements all grew faster than the operating profit did. The distinction between "AGEL shareholders' PAT" (+14.4%, genuinely good) and "group PAT including non-controlling interests" (flat) is not cosmetic: it is the difference between the operating story management leads with on calls and what the consolidated P&L actually shows for the year. §04 unpacks exactly what happened between EBITDA and the bottom line.

The 2030 target and the Khavda mega-project

AGEL's stated long-run target is 50 GW of renewable capacity plus 5 GW/30 GWh of pumped storage and 50 GWh of battery storage by 2030, up from 20.1 GW today. The centrepiece is Khavda, Gujarat — a planned 30 GW site spanning 538 sq km (management describes it as "nearly five times the size of Paris"), where operational capacity reached 10.3 GW as of Q1FY27 (up from 5.6 GW a year earlier), with management targeting the full 30 GW there by 2029. Within Khavda, AGEL has also built what it calls one of the world's largest single-location BESS deployments (3.55 GWh installed as of Q1FY27) and is developing a maiden 500 MW pumped-storage project at Chitravathi, Andhra Pradesh, targeted for commissioning within FY27.

The moat, as far as this dataset can verify it

Management repeatedly claims sector leadership and an "industry-leading" 91–94% EBITDA margin; no independent competitor benchmark or third-party market-share verification was found anywhere in this dataset, so that framing is self-reported and treated here as a claim, not a confirmed fact. What is independently verifiable: capacity-addition execution that genuinely outpaces peers on the metrics disclosed (5.1 GW added in FY26, described by management as "the highest greenfield annual capacity extension globally by any company outside China"); an inaugural investment-grade international credit rating (JCR BBB+/Stable, "equivalent to India's sovereign rating," per the April 2026 call); "Must Run" status for renewable generation under the Electricity Act, 2003; a disclosed US$3.4 billion revolving construction-financing facility intended to keep growth "fully funded"; and a large land bank (management cites ~250,000 acres of resource-rich sites). Whether that combination constitutes a durable moat versus simply being the best-capitalised first mover in a policy-driven, still-nascent Indian renewables buildout is a judgment call this dataset cannot fully settle independently — and §07–08 below show that AGEL's own group structure (its relationship with sister company Adani Energy Solutions Limited, AESL) is itself now a live variable in how much of that execution accrues to AGEL shareholders specifically.

PPA mix and realized pricing

As of the April 2026 call, AGEL's 19.3 GW operating base broke down as: 9.7 GW fully under long-term PPA; 5.3 GW classified "infirm" — PPA-designated capacity selling into the merchant market only until evacuation infrastructure is ready, at which point it converts automatically; and 4.2 GW pure merchant capacity awaiting long-term contracting. Total signed PPA pipeline stood at 28 GW. Management's blended average PPA rate across the book was ₹3.10/unit; new contracting guidance given in April was ₹2.60–2.80/unit for solar and ₹3.70–3.80/unit for wind. By the July Q1FY27 call, the benchmark rates cited for the new AESL offtake agreements had come in at roughly ₹2.7 (solar) and ₹3.4–3.5 (wind) — solar in line with April guidance, but wind roughly 5–8% below the range guided three months earlier, a modest miss worth flagging rather than smoothing over. Infirm/merchant realization in Q1FY27 was cited at roughly ₹2.5/unit, "including RECs."

Guidance vs. actual: capacity and BESS pace

⚠
Q1FY27 pace implies a steep back-half ramp is required on both frontsManagement guided (Apr 2026, reiterated Jul 2026) to 4.5–5.0 GW of FY27 greenfield capacity and "north of 10,000 MWh" (10+ GWh) of cumulative BESS capacity by FY27-end. Q1FY27 delivered only 848 MW of the ~4.5–5.0 GW annual target (roughly 17–19% of the low end, in the first of four quarters) and BESS capacity of 3.55 GWh cumulative — about a third of the 10+ GWh target. Management's own explanation ties both to evacuation-infrastructure timing (7 GW of new Khavda transmission capacity expected online by Dec 2026, another ~7 GW by Mar 2027) rather than execution capability — the company states its own execution capacity is 7–8 GW/year, well above the 4.5–5.0 GW it is choosing to build to avoid repeating last year's evacuation bottleneck. That explanation is plausible and consistent with management's own prior candour about curtailment (§08), but it is still a guided target that is currently running behind its own pacing after one quarter.

Segment mix, geography & expansion

Capacity mix as of FY26: 14.2 GW solar, 2.7 GW wind, 3.3 GW hybrid, spread across 12 Indian states — entirely domestic; no export revenue or new-country market entry was found in this dataset, despite two wholly-owned subsidiaries named "Adani Renewable Energy Middle East Limited" and "AGEL Global IFSC Limited" appearing in the FY26 consolidated entity list with no disclosed revenue. Horizontal expansion is clear: from solar/wind/hybrid generation into battery storage (BESS) and now pumped hydro storage (PSP, first project targeted for FY27). Vertical expansion is limited: AGEL develops and operates its own assets (via its in-house Energy Network Operations Center for O&M) but does not manufacture its own panels or turbines — module/cell manufacturing sits in a separate group entity (Mundra Solar Energy Limited appears in AGEL's own consolidated accounts only as an associate, not a subsidiary). AGEL's self-reported claim of a 14% share of India's utility-scale solar capacity (News, 1 Jul 2026) has no independent third-party verification in this dataset.

⚠
FY26 group PAT was flat despite 23% EBITDA growth — the single most important number in this sectionEBITDA from power supply grew 23% YoY in FY26, but consolidated Total Income grew only 11.2% (not 22% — that 22% figure is specific to the "power supply" revenue sub-line; broader Revenue from Operations grew 15.3% and Other Income actually fell 26%, from ₹1,210 Cr to ₹891 Cr), and group PAT including non-controlling interests was essentially flat (₹2,001 Cr → ₹1,987 Cr, −0.7%). Finance costs (+18.0%) and depreciation (+35.0%) both grew faster than the group's operating profit could absorb at the same pace all year. The one genuinely good offsetting fact: NCI's absolute share of profit fell 40% (₹557 Cr → ₹335 Cr FY25→FY26), which is why PAT attributable to AGEL shareholders specifically still grew a healthy 14.4% even as the group total didn't move. Whether NCI's declining share persists or reverses (new JV-partnered SPVs, e.g. the TotalEnergies joint ventures disclosed in the FY26 audit's Key Audit Matters, are still ramping) is worth watching each quarter.

The curtailment and merchant-realization drag, quantified by management

On the April 2026 call, Executive Director Sagar Adani gave an unusually specific number: AGEL lost ₹500 Cr of FY26 EBITDA to grid curtailment, plus a further ₹800–1,000 Cr from realizing below long-term contracted rates on infirm/merchant power — a combined ₹1,300–1,500 Cr EBITDA headwind for the year, which management at the time characterized largely as a "one-time" consequence of FY26's deliberately aggressive merchant-capacity build (done to lock in ISTS transmission-charge waivers before they expired). Three months later, on the July Q1FY27 call, curtailment was still cited as reducing EBITDA by 5–7% in the current quarter, with management guiding that the issue should ease only "by the end of this calendar year" — i.e., a real, ongoing cost, not fully resolved as the April framing implied, even if the company's own transcript is admirably candid that "I don't think anyone can say this issue is permanently behind us" (§08).

Deferred tax and minority-interest volatility add further quarter-to-quarter noise

Q1FY27's deferred tax line swung to a ₹214 Cr charge, versus a ₹12 Cr deferred tax credit in Q4FY26 — a meaningful, non-operating swing that dented reported EPS growth relative to pre-tax profit growth in the latest quarter. Combined with the NCI volatility above, AGEL's quarter-to-quarter EPS is driven by a genuinely wide set of below-EBITDA moving parts, not just the operating business — a pattern consistent with the persistent, large negative earnings surprises documented in §09.

Forward margin framing (management's own, not audited)

No explicit forward EBITDA-margin percentage target was given in either transcript beyond "sustaining industry-leading" levels via O&M efficiency. CFO Saurabh Shah did offer two specific, quantified forward metrics on the Q1FY27 call: a BESS EBITDA thumb-rule of ₹25–30 lakh per MWh of installed capacity, and a company-wide "run-rate" EBITDA figure of roughly ₹17,000 Cr currently, targeted at ₹21,000 Cr by FY27-end. Both are management's own annualized, forward-looking framing devices — not the audited quarterly/annual EBITDA reported in the P&L above — and this report treats them as directional guidance rather than comparable actuals.

The ₹7,012 Cr warrant conversion — the main driver of the improved equity base

In December 2023/January 2024, AGEL issued 6.31 crore warrants on a preferential basis to a promoter-group entity at ₹1,480.75/warrant (aggregate consideration ₹9,350 Cr). These were called up progressively and fully converted to equity shares during FY26, bringing in ₹7,012 Cr of cash (per the consolidated cash-flow statement), stated as used for debt repayment, subsidiary investment and general corporate purposes. This single transaction is the primary reason parent-attributable equity rose 64.5% YoY and the standalone Debt-Equity ratio improved from 2.32x to 1.30x (per the FY26 Annual Report's own Schedule-III ratio disclosure, standalone basis only) — a genuinely positive capital-structure event, but a one-off rather than a repeatable source of deleveraging.

●
Leverage is still growing quickly in absolute termsEven after the warrant conversion, consolidated total borrowings rose 27.0% YoY (₹77,476 Cr → ₹98,373 Cr) and net debt rose 28.4% — both broadly tracking the 29.4% asset growth, so relative leverage intensity hasn't obviously worsened, but the absolute debt load keeps climbing at nearly the same pace as the asset base, meaning continued market access for both debt and equity remains structurally necessary (§06). Supplier credit — effectively vendor-financed working capital — also jumped 255% (₹593 Cr → ₹2,105 Cr), an additional, less visible source of capex funding worth tracking.

Credit rating

AGEL secured an inaugural international rating of JCR BBB+/Stable from Japan Credit Rating Agency in FY26, described by management as "equivalent to India's sovereign credit rating" — a genuine capital-markets access milestone. Management stated on the April 2026 call that it expects "downward pressure" on its blended cost of debt (cited then at 8.9%) as a result, though no updated blended cost-of-debt figure was disclosed on the July Q1FY27 call.

This is a real, quantifiable divergence worth tracking, though nowhere near the scale of a receivables-driven red flag — AGEL's core business (long-term PPA power sales to DISCOMs/government-backed counterparties) does not carry the receivables-quality risk profile of, say, a project-milestone-billing business.

Capex growth (+5.3%) lagged capacity growth (+35% GW) and energy-sales growth (+34%) by a wide margin in FY26 — plausibly explained by a falling blended cost per MW as the mix shifts toward lower-cost solar-heavy Khavda capacity, or by a lag between capex spend and commissioning (spend in one year often capitalizes into capacity the following year), but this dataset cannot independently confirm which explanation dominates; it is presented here as an interesting pattern rather than an asserted cause.

Free cash flow has been negative in both years shown, funded by continuous debt issuance (₹37,391 Cr gross debt raised in FY26 against ₹19,905 Cr repaid) and the one-off ₹7,012 Cr warrant-conversion equity inflow. That is standard, even expected, for a renewable IPP in an aggressive multi-year capacity build-out — but it does mean AGEL's growth remains entirely externally funded, not self-funding, and no formal free-cash-flow or "self-funding by year X" target was found anywhere in either available transcript. Management's own forward cash commentary is expressed exclusively in EBITDA run-rate terms (§04), not FCF terms — a disclosure gap worth noting given how central continued capital-markets access is to the whole growth story, particularly against the backdrop of the still-unresolved US litigation matter (§07).

Other conference-call insights

Candid disclosure on curtailment risk, quantified battery-storage unit economics, and a group-wide division of labour crystallizing around AESL as the customer-facing entity.

That admission is worth weighing against the more upbeat framing used elsewhere on the same call and reiterated in the Q1FY27 call ("we do expect that by the end of this calendar year… there should not be any curtailment"). Both statements are on the record; the more cautious one is the more analytically useful, since it is the one management is willing to stand behind even under direct questioning.

AESL is becoming the group's customer-facing "energy solutions" layer

Multiple Q1FY27 answers converged on the same structural point: AGEL is deliberately stepping back from direct C&I/data-center customer relationships, ceding that role to AESL, while AGEL itself "concentrate[s] more on operational excellence, project execution, deploying the CapEx more efficiently." When asked about Adani Power's participation in a round-the-clock (RTC) renewable tender, management declined to comment on a sister company but confirmed the group's stated principle: individual generating companies (like AGEL) supply capacity, while AESL blends sources and manages the end-customer/DISCOM relationship. This is a coherent group strategy, but it also means AGEL's own investor-facing growth story increasingly depends on AESL's separate ability to contract that capacity out — a dependency worth tracking via AESL's own disclosures going forward, not just AGEL's.

Structural demand tailwind flagged, not yet in the numbers

Both AGEL management (Q1FY27 call) and Morgan Stanley's initiation note (§02, §09) cite rising AI/data-center-driven power demand as a structural tailwind. No AGEL disclosure in this dataset yet quantifies data-center-specific contracted volume or revenue — it remains a forward narrative rather than a booked number as of Q1FY27.

Bottom line

What's good: execution at a scale few peers globally can match — 5.1 GW of greenfield capacity added in FY26, the largest outside China for the second consecutive year, with Q1FY27 sustaining 27% capacity growth and 29%/33% revenue/EBITDA growth; an industry-leading, if self-reported, 91–94% EBITDA margin; a genuine capital-structure win in the ₹7,012 Cr promoter warrant conversion that materially improved the standalone debt-equity ratio; an inaugural investment-grade international credit rating (JCR BBB+); AGEL-shareholder-attributable PAT that actually grew a healthy 14.4% in FY26 even as the consolidated group total was flat; a majority-independent board; a clean, unmodified audit opinion across every period reviewed; and management that is, on the record, unusually candid about curtailment risk not being "permanently behind" the business even while also promoting a more upbeat public narrative.

What's not good: consolidated group PAT was essentially flat in FY26 (−0.7%) despite 23% EBITDA growth, as finance costs and depreciation grew faster than operating profit could absorb; one of the last five quarters (Q3FY26) printed an outright parent-level loss; free cash flow has been deeply negative in both FY25 and FY26, funded entirely by continued debt and equity issuance with no formal FCF or self-funding target disclosed; curtailment cost an admitted ₹1,300–1,500 Cr of FY26 EBITDA and remains a 5–7% ongoing drag as of Q1FY27; both FY27 capacity/BESS pace and the FY27 capex run-rate are tracking behind their own guided schedules after one quarter; consolidated total borrowings grew 27% YoY even after the warrant-conversion equity infusion; no consolidated Schedule-III leverage/coverage ratio table exists in the source, only a standalone one that materially understates the group's true project-level leverage; a large, newly disclosed related-party restructuring has locked ~4 GW of previously open capacity into 25-year fixed-price contracts with sister company AESL, which at least one independent analyst reads as a direct transfer of shareholder upside; a US DOJ/SEC matter against certain directors remains formally unresolved even as it trends toward settlement, alongside a separate, live Supreme Court petition over alleged state-contract bribery; and the stock has missed consensus quarterly EPS in every one of the last six reported quarters.

How management has navigated it: candidly on operating risk (the curtailment admission, the detailed BESS-economics disclosure, the transparent quantification of the FY26 EBITDA headwind) but with noticeably less transparency on group-level profit quality — neither transcript in this dataset directly addresses why consolidated PAT was flat in a 23%-EBITDA-growth year, why Q3FY26 swung to a loss, or how the AESL restructuring's pricing was benchmarked beyond "arm's length." The company has also chosen, in this same window, to convert a large share of its open/merchant upside into fixed-price related-party contracts precisely when merchant realizations and AI/data-center demand tailwinds are becoming more topical — a strategic choice management frames as risk reduction and at least one major broker reads as value transfer, and this report cannot fully adjudicate between the two from the data available.

AI-Synthesized ViewLikelihood the next 12 months resolve in the bulls' favour from today's starting price: moderate, roughly 45–55%. The physical execution story is real and independently corroborated by multiple fresh bull initiations (Morgan Stanley, Axis, Elara) built on management's own capacity/BESS targets. But this report's own tabulated numbers show a materially bumpier underlying business than the headline growth rates suggest: flat consolidated group profit in FY26, a genuine loss quarter within the trailing twelve months, persistently negative free cash flow, capacity and capex pacing that is already behind its own FY27 schedule after one quarter, a still-open US legal matter, and a related-party restructuring an independent broker has explicitly flagged as value-diluting. None of these is individually disqualifying for a business at this stage of a capital-intensive infrastructure build-out — but together they explain why the Street itself is split between an ₹980 bear case and an ₹1,800 bull case on the same set of facts, and why this report leans toward the base case (§10) rather than either extreme.
