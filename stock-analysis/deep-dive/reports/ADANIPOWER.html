Equity Research Note · Power Generation / Independent Power Producer (Thermal)

Adani Power Limited

NSE: ADANIPOWER · BSE: 533096 · FY2023–FY2027(Q1) performance review, BSE-filing and earnings-call synthesis, as of 22 August 2026.

The one-line story: India's largest private thermal IPP (18,150 MW) is mid-way through a genuinely credible ~₹2 lakh crore capacity supercycle — 23,720 MW under construction toward a target raised mid-year from 42 GW to 45 GW by FY2031-32, with 95% of existing capacity and 56% of the pipeline already tied up under long-term PPAs, a CARE rating upgrade to AA+ on 18 Aug 2026, and a blockbuster Q1 FY27 (revenue +27%, PAT to shareholders +42%). But the capex ramp has already flipped free cash flow negative in FY26 for the first time in this data set, net debt rose to ₹58,381 crore by June 2026, a ₹15,000 crore dilutive QIP was approved days after Q1 results, unit-commissioning dates have slipped by roughly two quarters twice in a row, and a chunk of the headline PAT-growth numbers management cites in prepared remarks (47% for Q1FY27, 64% for Q4FY26) are the larger pre-minority-interest figures rather than the smaller to-shareholder numbers EPS is actually based on (42% and 52% respectively) — both fully disclosed, but easy to conflate if you only listen to the call.

01

What Adani Power does, and how it has grown

A 1988-founded, Adani Group thermal-generation platform that has become India's largest private IPP largely through PPA-anchored brownfield and greenfield build-out plus opportunistic distressed-asset acquisitions.

Adani Power Limited generates, transmits and sells electricity from a combined installed and commissioned capacity of 18,150 MW as of FY26 — almost entirely coal-based thermal, spread across Mundra (4,620 MW, Gujarat), Tiroda (3,300 MW, Maharashtra), Kawai (1,320 MW, Rajasthan), Udupi (1,200 MW, Karnataka), Raigarh/Korba (600 MW, Chhattisgarh), Raipur (1,370 MW, Chhattisgarh), Mahan (1,200 MW, Madhya Pradesh), Tuticorin/Coastal Energen (1,200 MW, Tamil Nadu), Mirzapur (1,600 MW, Uttar Pradesh), Dahanu (500 MW, Maharashtra) and Godda (1,600 MW, Jharkhand, exporting to Bangladesh) — plus a small 40 MW solar plant at Bitta, Gujarat (MarketData/overview.md). Revenue is sold under long-term PPAs, supplemental PPAs, medium/short-term PPAs and on the merchant market, with a secondary coal-trading/import business. Chairman is Gautam Adani; Managing Director is Anil Sardana; day-to-day operations are run by Whole-time Director & CEO S. B. Khyalia and CFO Dilip Jha (2026-05-29 annual report; earnings-call transcripts).

Read plainly, this is not a smooth growth story: FY24's blowout −PAT +110%, EBITDA margin spiking to 55.5%− was substantially a one-off. Group finance-income data shows FY24 carried a ₹5,587 crore positive net-interest swing (Total Revenue table, MarketData) tied to the regulatory carrying-cost recognitions detailed across the 22 Jul 2026 financial-results notes (Karnataka, Tiroda, Kawai, Udupi coal-cost compensation orders). Strip that out and FY24's "core" growth was real but far less dramatic than the headline EPS jump implies — which is exactly why FY25 EPS look like a decline (−37.4%) when it was actually FY24 that was unusually flattered. FY26 growth then normalised further: revenue fell 3.5% on a subdued demand year (management's own description, see §03), while EBITDA and PAT held roughly flat — evidence the two-part PPA tariff structure (fixed capacity charge + pass-through energy charge) cushions earnings even when volumes/merchant prices soften.

The FY26–FY32 pivot: from a stalled-out generator to a 45 GW growth platform

Following a long period (FY19–FY23) where capacity additions were mostly distressed-asset acquisitions rather than new-build, Adani Power's FY2025-26 Annual Report frames a targeted capacity of 41,870 MW by FY2031-32 (23,720 MW of new/expansion capacity on top of the existing 18,150 MW), funded by an estimated ₹2 lakh crore capex programme (Integrated Annual Report, "Letter from the MD and CEO"). By the Q1FY27 call (23 Jul 2026), management had raised this long-run target again, to 45 GW, citing the newly acquired Jaiprakash Power Ventures stake and an additional 3 GW "planning bucket" for anticipated state PPA bids. Execution is substantially de-risked on paper: the entire 24 GW of boiler-turbine-generator (BTG) equipment for the expansion has already been ordered, and land has been secured for the full pipeline (Q1FY27 call).

Inorganic growth: Vidarbha, Coastal Energen/Moxie, and the Jaiprakash Associates resolution

Alongside organic build-out, FY26–FY27 saw three distinct distressed-asset acquisitions completed via India's Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code process: Vidarbha Industries Power Limited (600 MW, Maharashtra) became a wholly owned subsidiary 7 Jul 2025 for a ₹4,000 crore upfront lender payment; Coastal Energen (1,200 MW, Tamil Nadu, now Moxie Power Generation Ltd) was acquired via consortium and consolidated as a subsidiary with the Company holding a 49% stake but de-facto control; and in the Jaiprakash Associates Limited resolution (NCLT-approved 17 Mar 2026), Adani Power acquired a 24% stake in listed Jaiprakash Power Ventures Ltd (JPVL, for ₹2,993 crore, accounted as an associate) plus the 180 MW Churk plant and an 11.49% stake in Prayagraj Power Generation Company (for ₹1,200 crore) — both agreements signed 20 May 2026 (Q1FY27 financial-results filing, note 22). This pattern — buying stressed thermal capacity cheaply through CIRP rather than only greenfield-building — has been a recurring, capital-efficient growth lever for the company across several years.

The moat: contracted revenue, scale, and brownfield capital efficiency

95% of existing operating capacity (18.15 GW) is tied up under long-term/medium-term PPAs as of Q4FY26/Q1FY27, up from roughly 84% at the start of FY26 (merchant capacity fell from 16% to 5% of the portfolio over the year, per the Apr-2026 call) — a genuine and verifiable de-risking of the revenue base, not a self-reported claim. Scale (largest private thermal generator in India, per the MD's own annual-report letter) gives negotiating leverage in coal linkage/SHAKTI allocations and PPA bidding. A high share of brownfield expansion (adding units at existing sites like Korba, Raipur, Raigarh and Mahan) is explicitly cited by management as improving capital efficiency and shortening commissioning timelines versus pure greenfield builds. No independent market-share or competitor-benchmark figure was found in this dataset beyond management's own "largest private thermal IPP" framing, which is a familiar and plausible but self-reported claim.

How to read the commissioning slippageManagement's own framing (IR head Nishit Dave, Apr-2026 call) is that unit-level dates are "deferring by an average of 6 months" spread across the multi-year pipeline, with the aggregate FY31-32 target unaffected — and the fact that the long-run target was raised (42→45 GW) rather than cut two calls later is a real, checkable data point supporting that framing. Geography, diversification & new lines

All confirmed FY26–FY27 order/PPA wins in this dataset are domestic (Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Bangladesh export via Godda). Horizontal/vertical expansion beyond core coal thermal is real but still nascent: an SPV incorporated for a 570 MW Bhutan hydro project (PPA terms not yet finalised as of Jul-2026); multiple Adani Atomic Energy Limited subsidiaries incorporated between Feb and Jun 2026 for prospective nuclear power projects (target raised from 5 GW to 10 GW by 2035 on the Q1FY27 call, entirely contingent on government rules not yet notified under the relevant Act); and continued small-scale coal-trading. None of the hydro or nuclear initiatives contribute revenue yet, and management was explicit on both calls that capital allocation and timelines for nuclear cannot be finalised until the Government of India issues implementing rules.

The "which PAT number" problem

Adani Power's consolidated P&L reports two distinct profit figures each quarter: total group net profit (before splitting out minority/non-controlling interests) and profit attributable to the Company's own shareholders (the smaller number, and the one diluted EPS is calculated from). On both of the last two calls, management's prepared remarks cited the larger, group-wide growth rate:

Both figures are fully and correctly disclosed in the filed results tables — this is not misstatement. But a listener relying only on the spoken headline in either call would overstate shareholder profit growth by 5–12 percentage points. Non-controlling interest itself jumped sharply in FY26 (from ₹8.5 Cr in Q3FY26 to ₹254 Cr in Q4FY26) because Moxie Power's Unit 2 signed a new PPA with Tamil Nadu mid-year, converting a previously merchant, wholly-attributed unit into one where 51% of profit is now carved out to minority shareholders — a real, disclosed mechanical driver, not a red flag in itself, but the reason group and to-shareholder growth rates now diverge more than they used to.

Other one-off items worth separating from the run-rate

  • Q4FY26 tax: Reuters headlined the quarter's profit jump as driven by "a one-time tax gain" (29 Apr 2026); the filed tax-provision line shows current tax of just ₹112.58 Cr in the March-2026 quarter versus ₹899.18 Cr a year earlier and ₹456.93 Cr the prior quarter — a genuinely light tax quarter that inflated Q4FY26 PAT growth beyond what operating performance alone would explain.
  • Q1FY27 prior-period revenue: ₹1,386 Cr of "reported" revenue/EBITDA in Q1FY27 was a one-time net recognition of prior-period revenue (retrospective GUVNL base-rate revision effective back to Oct 2018). Management was transparent about this, consistently distinguishing "continuing" (₹17,936 Cr total income, EBITDA ₹6,983 Cr, +22% YoY) from "reported" (₹19,322 Cr, EBITDA ₹8,369 Cr, +36% YoY) throughout the call — the continuing basis is the more decision-useful figure for run-rate purposes.

Forward margin guidance

No explicit forward EBITDA-margin target was given on either call. The one long-dated marker offered: CEO Khyalia's answer to an analyst question that consolidated EBITDA should "conservatively" reach ₹50,000 Cr by FY2031, "or even 2030" in a best case — against FY26 continuing EBITDA of ₹21,285 Cr, that implies roughly a 19–24% EBITDA CAGR over the intervening years (this report's own computation from the stated target, not a management-disclosed CAGR). HDFC Sky's post-Q1FY27 note independently estimates 18.8%/19.2% revenue/EBITDA CAGR through FY29 — a reasonably close external cross-check on the lower end of that range.

Funding the gap: NCDs, bank debt, and now a ₹15,000 Cr QIP

The Board approved raising up to ₹15,000 crore via QIP or other permissible equity mode on 22 Jul 2026 (same meeting as Q1FY27 results), alongside raising the Companies Act borrowing-limit ceiling from ₹75,000 Cr to ₹1,00,000 Cr; both were ratified by shareholders at a nine-minute EGM on 14 Aug 2026 (99%+ approval implied by the brevity, though exact vote tallies were not captured in this filing). No issue price, timeline or use-of-proceeds breakdown beyond "supporting the 45 GW expansion" has been disclosed as of this report's date — this is an enabling resolution, not an executed raise, and CFO Dilip Jha was explicit on the Q1FY27 call that the company would "come out with our plans... as and when there is a need." At the current market cap of ₹396,300 Cr, a ₹15,000 Cr raise implies roughly 3.5–4% dilution depending on issue pricing — modest in isolation, but a genuine signal that internal accruals alone are no longer sufficient to fund the pace of expansion management has committed to (confirmed directly in §06).

Contingent liabilities: modest and shrinking; capital commitments: large and doubling

Total disclosed contingent liabilities (claims not acknowledged as debts — income tax, customs duty, transmission-line relinquishment, GST, water-charge penalties) fell from ₹1,140.7 Cr (Mar-2025) to ₹707.3 Cr (Mar-2026), a genuinely reassuring trend relative to a ₹1.4 lakh crore-plus balance sheet (Annual Report, Note 47(a)). These are overwhelmingly small, long-running regulatory/tax disputes (Raipur TPP customs classification, Mundra TPP CST matter, Godda water-charge penalty) rather than anything balance-sheet-threatening. The much larger number to watch is capital commitments — ₹78,567 Cr of contracted-but-unexecuted capex as at Mar-2026, nearly double the ₹40,126 Cr a year earlier — which is the real forward funding obligation implied by the 45 GW build-out.

Credit rating trajectory: a genuine, independently verified positive

CARE Ratings upgraded Adani Power to AA+/Stable across its entire ₹90,500 Cr rated debt portfolio on 18 Aug 2026, up from AA/Stable, citing sustained financial and operational performance, diversified portfolio, PPA-backed revenue visibility, improved fuel security and a strengthened capital structure. The Annual Report separately notes an "eight-notch upgrade since 2020" across all four major domestic rating agencies (CRISIL, ICRA, India Ratings, CARE) to AA/Stable as the pre-Aug-2026 baseline — this CARE action is the most recent step beyond that baseline. A rising credit rating during a period of rising absolute debt is a meaningful, third-party-verified signal that lenders view the leverage increase as well-matched to contracted cash flows, not as deteriorating credit quality.

Other call & disclosure insights worth watching

Nuclear and international hydro remain pre-revenue optionality; the Bangladesh receivable situation is genuinely improving; and a RE-RTC "trading platform" structure with sister company PTSL adds a layer of complexity worth understanding before assuming it changes Adani Power's own risk profile.

Bangladesh (Godda/BPDB) receivables: real improvement, still unresolved on the disputed portion

Godda TPP's export PPA with Bangladesh Power Development Board has been a multi-year overhang. Management confirmed on the Q1FY27 call that receivables have "significantly reduced" quarter-on-quarter, running at roughly USD 100 million/month in regular collections against a ~USD 400 million balance as of June-2026 (down from an "all-time high" a year earlier). The disputed portion of the receivable remains under a Singapore International Arbitration Centre-appointed expert process, with both parties having signed an appointment agreement as of the Apr-2026 call — a formal resolution mechanism now in motion, but not yet concluded. Company policy, confirmed by the CEO, is to recognise as revenue only the undisputed portion.

The RE-RTC "trading platform" with PTSL: added complexity, contained risk to Adani Power itself

A 2,500 MW round-the-clock (RTC) renewable-energy supply contract with MSEDCL, intermediated through a group trading platform (referenced as PTSL, an Adani Energy-linked entity), drew repeated analyst questions across both calls. Management's clarification: Adani Power's own contribution is limited to small residual/untied thermal capacity (estimated at 1–1.5% of its portfolio) supplied into the platform at ordinary PPA-like capacity-charge economics; the requirement to blend in renewable/battery/pumped-storage sources to make the supply "round-the-clock" is an exposure borne by PTSL as the intermediary, not by Adani Power directly. On the facts disclosed, this looks like a modest, well-structured revenue contribution rather than a hidden risk transfer — but the multiple rounds of analyst clarification needed across two consecutive calls suggest the structure is not fully intuitive even to close followers of the stock.

Nuclear and Bhutan hydro: real optionality, zero revenue contribution, no committed capital yet

Management raised its own nuclear ambition mid-year (5 GW → 10 GW by 2035) and has incorporated multiple Adani Atomic Energy Limited subsidiaries, but was explicit on both calls that technology choice (domestic NPCIL vs. imported), site finalisation, and capital allocation are all contingent on government rules not yet notified under the relevant Act — management's own estimate of execution time from planning to commissioning is "at least 5 years" once rules are issued. The 570 MW Bhutan hydro SPV is similarly pre-PPA. Both are credible long-dated optionality worth tracking but should not be modelled into any FY27–FY29 earnings forecast.

Brand recognition

Adani Power was ranked India's most valued energy brand by Brand Finance (USD 1.8 billion brand value, AAA brand rating) per the Q1FY27 call — a third-party validation of brand strength, though brand-value rankings of this kind are a soft signal relative to the hard operating and financial data elsewhere in this report.

Bottom line

What's good: 95% of existing capacity and 56% of the under-construction pipeline are already tied up under long-term PPAs — the single strongest, most independently verifiable de-risking fact in this report; capex has run 5.1x depreciation in FY26, unambiguous evidence of real capacity build rather than financial engineering; CFO has tracked or exceeded PAT in three of the last four years with no earnings-quality red flag; contingent liabilities are small and shrinking; the CARE upgrade to AA+ on the entire ₹90,500 Cr debt book is a genuine, independently verified credit-quality positive; a professionally-run executive team (MD, CEO, CFO distinct from the Adani family Chairman) delivers detailed, generally transparent disclosure, including proactively flagging its own commissioning delays; the long-run capacity target was raised (42→45 GW), not cut, even as near-term unit dates slipped; and Q1FY27 delivered the company's strongest quarter on record on genuinely strong demand, not accounting effects alone.

What's not good: free cash flow turned negative in FY26 for the first time in this data set and will very likely stay negative through FY27–28 on guided capex; net debt/equity has already reversed its multi-year decline and is rising again; a ₹15,000 Cr dilutive QIP was approved days after a blowout quarter, with no price or timeline yet disclosed; unit-level commissioning dates (Korba, Mahan) have slipped by roughly two quarters on two consecutive calls; management's own spoken PAT-growth figures on both recent calls used the larger, pre-minority-interest group number rather than the smaller to-shareholder figure EPS is based on (a 5–12 percentage-point gap both times); FY24's headline profitability was substantially inflated by one-off regulatory income recognition, meaning several multi-year growth comparisons look worse than the underlying business actually is; and a non-executive director's US DOJ/SEC matter, while not naming the Company, remains formally unresolved pending EDNY court action.

How management has navigated it: candidly on operational specifics — volunteering commissioning delays, consistently separating "continuing" from "reported" figures, and giving direct, numbers-backed answers to detailed analyst questions on tariffs, PLF and funding mix across two full transcripts. Less carefully on framing — leading with the larger pre-minority PAT growth number in prepared remarks twice running, and pairing the QIP approval announcement with the same board meeting as strong Q1 results, a sequencing that tends to soften how a dilutive raise reads. Neither is dishonest (every underlying figure is disclosed correctly in the filed tables), but both reward reading the primary filings rather than relying on the spoken headline.

AI-Synthesized ViewLikelihood the next 12 months resolve in the bulls' favour from today's starting price: moderate, roughly 45–55%. The contracted-revenue base and capacity roadmap are genuinely strong and independently checkable, and Q1FY27 shows the operating business executing well on demand — but the stock is no longer cheap (27.8x trailing, 6.1x book), free cash flow has just turned negative for the first time in this data set, a dilutive raise is pending with no disclosed terms, and the commissioning-slippage pattern needs to stop repeating for the FY31-32 target to stay credible on its current, twice-revised timeline. This is a "quality growth story, already partly priced" setup more than a clear mispricing in either direction.
