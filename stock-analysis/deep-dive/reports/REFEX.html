Equity Research Note · Ash & Coal Logistics / Wind Turbine Manufacturing / Green Mobility

Refex Industries Limited

NSE: REFEX · BSE: 532884 · FY2023–FY2026 performance review, quarterly investor-presentation and earnings-call synthesis, and forward scenario framework, as of 17 August 2026.

The one-line story: Refex has spent FY26 shedding a volatile legacy trading book (refrigerant gas, power trading — both now discontinued) and re-basing itself around three focused engines — the profitable, cash-generative core (fly-ash & coal logistics for thermal power plants), a fast-scaling but still loss-making wind-turbine manufacturing bet (Venwind), and a green-mobility business now being demerged into a separately listed entity. Revenue and PAT have both roughly doubled year-on-year and debt has fallen even as the balance sheet tripled in size — but free cash flow has been negative for four straight years running, receivable days have nearly tripled, and the company's own auditor flags impairment risk on receivables now equal to 58% of total assets. Management guidance has also proven directionally right but numerically slippery: an ash-volume ramp promised "this year" has already been pushed to Q4FY27, and the wind subsidiary posted an EBITDA loss in its first full commercial quarter just weeks after being pitched as "better than industry" margin.

01

What Refex does, and how it has grown

A largest-in-its-niche logistics business for thermal power plants, now funding two much more capital-hungry, unproven bets on top of it.

Incorporated in 2002 as Refex Refrigerants Limited and renamed Refex Industries Limited in November 2013, the Chennai-headquartered company has spent the past two years deliberately narrowing its focus. Two legacy, low-margin trading lines — Power Trading (discontinued August 2025) and the original Refrigerant Gas business (discontinued January 2026) — have been wound down. What remains, per the Q1FY27 segment note, is three businesses: Ash & Coal Handling (fly-ash/pond-ash excavation, transport and disposal, plus coal-yard logistics, for thermal power plants — run out of the standalone parent), Wind (5.3MW wind-turbine-generator manufacturing via 73.28%-owned subsidiary Venwind Refex Power Ltd, "VRPL"), and Green Mobility (EV-led corporate transport via Refex Green Mobility Ltd, "RGML" — being demerged into a separately listed Refex Mobility Ltd, see §8).

Group structure & the three-entity naming trap

Promoter entity Refex Holding Private Limited (formerly Sherisha Technologies) holds 56.84% of RIL (MarketData). The wider Refex group also includes Refex Renewables & Infrastructure Limited, a separately listed company that also reported Q1 results the same week as RIL (see §2) — it is not a subsidiary consolidated into any figure in this report. Once the pending demerger completes, Refex Mobility Limited will become a fourth, independently listed entity carrying the green-mobility business out of RIL's own consolidated numbers.

The moat, as management frames it

On the June 2026 earnings call, Chairman & MD Anil Jain described Refex as India's largest organized ash & coal handling player, active across 40 thermal plants (42 by the August call) in 14–15 states, with the next-closest competitor at roughly "25% of its scale" — a claim management itself qualified as self-reported ("no exact data, not listed"). The stated addressable market was put at ~₹75,000 Cr with Refex at only ~3% national share in June, but reframed in August as "30–35% market penetration" — a different denominator (share of plants actually served vs. share of the national ash-handling opportunity) that is worth reading carefully rather than treating as a contradiction. On Wind, the moat argument is structural rather than scale-based: VRPL positions itself as a pure turbine supplier, not an EPC contractor building whole wind farms — an asset-light stance that caps its own execution risk on any single wind farm — and its 5.3MW ALMM-certified turbine is marketed as India's largest-capacity wind turbine platform, with VRPL calling itself the first company certified at that capacity.

04

Margins, expenses & profitability

A genuine FY26 margin expansion driven by exiting low-quality trading revenue, followed by a Q1FY27 compression that management attributes to a mix shift and a one-time cost — both plausible, neither yet proven over more than one quarter.

What moved FY26

Consolidated EBITDA margin rose from 12.2% (FY25) to 18.4% (FY26, yfinance) even as headline revenue grew only 1.0% — consistent with the "exit low-margin trading, keep the higher-margin core" narrative discussed in §01. PAT margin improved from 7.1% to 8.9% over the same period. On the June call, management attributed the Q4FY26 EBITDA margin spike specifically (reported as high as 20–22% on a standalone basis) to COGS falling 18% YoY on cost efficiency and tech-driven manpower reduction, plus the ongoing exit from low-margin trading lines — and explicitly flagged that print as an outlier versus the 15–18% blended band it considers sustainable.

What moved Q1FY27 specifically

A one-time refinancing charge. Management attributed the core (ex-Wind) margin dip versus Q4FY26 largely to a one-time ~₹4 Cr bank processing charge tied to refinancing a loan with Indian Overseas Bank at better terms — framing underlying core margin as "close to the same" once that charge is excluded.
Wind's first commercial quarter came in at a loss. Q1FY27 Wind EBITDA was −₹34 lakh on ₹297 Cr of revenue — a near-zero margin that diluted the blended consolidated print even before the refinancing charge, and a direct contradiction of the June call's "better than industry" margin framing for the segment.
Diesel cost pass-through is only partial. Escalation clauses recover an estimated 40–60% of diesel cost increases in the Ash & Coal business, per the June call — the remainder is absorbed, a real and recurring margin-sensitivity factor tied to fuel prices that is not fully hedged by contract structure.
Finance costs, historically modest but rising. Quarterly finance/interest expense has climbed from ₹5.15 Cr (Mar-25) to ₹6.17 Cr (Jun-25) to ₹9.14 Cr (Dec-25) to ₹10.15 Cr (Mar-26) to ₹12.92 Cr (Jun-26) — source: MarketData quarterly — tracking the working-capital intensity discussed in §06 even as total reported debt has fallen (§05), a combination that points to short-term working-capital borrowing (LCs/BGs) rather than long-term debt as the driver.

Forward margin guidance

Blended consolidated EBITDA margin is guided at "15% to 18%" on an ongoing basis (June call), with Wind targeted to reach only a 5–6% net margin by FY27 year-end (from a Q1 loss), and full turbine-industry-benchmark margins (18–20% EBITDA) only expected "2 years down the line" once component localization is complete — blade manufacturing in-house within 6–12 months, ~85% component localization within 12 months. Net effect for modelling purposes: expect blended margin to compress somewhat from FY26's unusually high 18.4% print toward the 15–18% band as Wind's larger, lower-margin revenue base dilutes the mix through FY27, with a Wind-led margin recovery only becoming visible in FY28 on management's own timeline.

Capital allocation: two large equity raises, and a capex pool quietly redirected

Two preferential issues funded the FY25–26 equity build-up: a March 2024 promoter issue (₹220 Cr raised, ₹184.69 Cr utilized, ₹35 Cr parked in an inter-corporate deposit) and an October 2024 issue envisaged at ₹927.81 Cr but which actually yielded ₹513.38 Cr after equity undersubscription and warrant lapse/forfeiture (₹130.69 Cr forfeited) — a soft investor-demand signal at the time that is easy to miss if only the headline "~₹927 Cr QIP" figure (repeated in some news coverage) is taken at face value. CARE Ratings, as the independent monitoring agency, reported nil deviation from stated objects for both issues as of 30 Jun 2026. However, of the ₹19.68 Cr originally earmarked from the 2024 preferential-issue pool specifically for capex, only ₹0.62 Cr had been utilized by June 2026 — the board proposed (and the 4 Aug 2026 AGM approved, 99.97% support) reallocating the unused ₹19.07 Cr from capex to general working capital. That is a small absolute sum, but it is a directionally consistent signal with the working-capital-hungry growth pattern flagged in §06: capex from this specific pool is running behind plan and cash is being redirected toward funding receivables/working capital instead.

⚠
Earnings-quality check: fails clean, and the company's own auditor agreesPAT has grown every year since FY24 while cash conversion has been erratic-to-poor: CFO/PAT ranged from 24% down to a deeply negative −157% (FY25) before recovering to a still-partial 53% in FY26, and free cash flow has been negative in all four years shown. The mechanical driver is receivables: receivable days rose from 55 (FY23) to 81 (FY24) to 114 (FY25) to 149 (FY26) — nearly tripling while revenue grew only ~1.4x over the same period — and the annual cash-flow statement's "Change in Receivables" line has been a growing cash drag every year (−₹169 Cr, −₹64 Cr, −₹482 Cr, −₹789 Cr, FY23→FY26). This is not just a ratio-derived concern: the FY26 Annual Report's own Key Audit Matters section flags impairment risk on ₹1,413.2 Cr of high-value receivables and contract assets in the Ash & Coal Handling segment — 58.3% of total consolidated assets — an independent, auditor-sourced confirmation of the same pattern this report's own ratio work surfaced. Payable days have also stretched materially (17 days FY24 → 92 days FY26), meaning working capital is being partly funded by leaning on suppliers as well as by the equity raises in §05.

Management's own framing (June call): receivables are guided at 105–125 days on a standalone basis, Wind's receivable cycle is described as letter-of-credit-backed and much shorter, and the ~₹700+ Cr of contract assets on the balance sheet were characterized as "normal unbilled revenue" — i.e., a structural feature of billing government/PSU thermal-plant counterparties on a bill-then-collect cycle, not a quality issue. That is a plausible explanation for a logistics business serving state-owned power plants, and it is worth noting neither call's analyst panel challenged it directly despite it being, by a wide margin, the single largest quantitative flag in this entire dataset. Readers should weight the auditor's independent impairment-risk flag (above) at least as heavily as management's own framing when assessing this line.

Read plainly: FY26's CFO turning positive (₹107 Cr, from FY25's −₹248.5 Cr) is a genuine, meaningful improvement and should not be dismissed — but free cash flow remains solidly negative, the receivables base keeps growing in absolute terms, and until collections meaningfully outpace revenue growth for at least two consecutive periods, the safest assumption is that further capex and working-capital needs will continue to be funded externally (equity, debt, or drawing on the credit-rating headroom from §02/§05) rather than from organic operating cash generation.

Other insights from the calls and disclosures

The green-mobility demerger mechanics, a reporting-comparability caveat an analyst flagged (and this report inherits), and a same-day investor-deck correction.

The Refex Mobility demerger: mechanics and status

The composite scheme — Refex Green Mobility Ltd (transferor) amalgamating into Refex Industries (demerged company), followed by demerger of the combined green-mobility business into Refex Mobility Limited (resulting company) — was approved by the RIL board on 22 Sep 2025. BSE and NSE issued "No Adverse Observation" letters on 16 Mar 2026; the scheme petition was filed with NCLT Chennai on 26 Mar 2026; the NCLT's order directing court-convened meetings was issued 18 Jun 2026 (RML and RGML shareholder meetings were dispensed with; RIL equity shareholder and creditor meetings, plus RGML creditor meetings, were required). Those meetings were held 5 Aug 2026; the approval threshold is 75% in value under Companies Act §230(6), with a 31 Jul 2026 voting cut-off date. As of this dataset's 14 Aug 2026 cutoff, the actual voting/approval percentages had not yet been disclosed — the scrutinizer's report was due to NCLT within three days of the meeting. Stated rationale (MD&A): "create two focused businesses with independent growth strategies and enhanced value creation potential." Once effective, RML shares will list on BSE/NSE, and Green Mobility's revenue and losses (₹33.0 Cr revenue, −₹13.6 Cr EBIT in Q1FY27 alone) will exit RIL's consolidated numbers — management's August-call guidance was that mobility-segment losses should be out of RIL's P&L by the end of Q3FY27.

A reporting-comparability caveat, direct from an analyst

On the August call, analyst Miten Shah pointed out that RIL currently reports standalone-only headline comparatives (i.e., the Ash & Coal core, excluding Wind) in some of its period-over-period framing, and asked whether this risks obscuring the true consolidated growth picture. Management's response was that this is for "apples-to-apples" comparability given Wind's near-zero prior-year base, and confirmed that full consolidated reporting as the primary comparative basis will only begin from Q4FY27. This report necessarily mixes standalone and consolidated figures across sections (each is labelled at point of use) for the same reason — readers should treat any single-quarter comparison with the same caution the analyst raised, at least until Q4FY27's fully consolidated comparatives are available.

Wind's competitive framing shifted between calls

In June, management's moat argument was specific and checkable in principle: the nearest ash/coal-handling competitor was pegged at roughly "25% of Refex's scale" (self-reported, unverified). In August, when a similar competitive-positioning question came up for Wind, management's answer was considerably softer — "competition will help... it is not a challenge, it will be an opportunity" — a framing this report flags as a non-answer rather than new information, worth noting alongside the more substantive TAM/penetration figures discussed in §01.

A minor but notable data-quality slip

Two Q1FY27 investor presentations were filed on the same day (29 Jul 2026, 74 minutes apart). The first (filed 15:57 IST) showed Gross Profit of ₹495.1 Cr on the standalone P&L slide — a figure inconsistent with the revenue and cost lines on the same slide. A "Revised Investor Presentation" filed at 17:11 IST corrected this to ₹124.1 Cr (consistent with Revenue ₹619.3 Cr less Materials ₹500.4 Cr plus the inventory-change adjustment). No other differences were found across either deck. A minor, quickly-corrected slip on its own, but one more data point in this report's overall read that Refex's public disclosures, while generally substantive, have shown a pattern of numbers needing in-period correction or clarification (alongside the margin-guidance clarification and the ash-volume-target revision above).

Bottom line

What's good: a real, deliberate strategic reset — exiting volatile refrigerant-gas and power-trading revenue in favour of a focused, profitable Ash & Coal core with a steady drumbeat of new order wins (~₹172 Cr across six contracts in this dataset window alone) and a growing order book (₹1,635 Cr); genuine deleveraging (debt/equity down from 0.48x to 0.15x, an independent Acuité rating upgrade); an asset-light Wind positioning (product supply, not EPC) that caps project-level execution risk; a clean, unmodified audit opinion with no auditor change; nil deviation on preferential-issue fund utilization per an independent monitoring agency; a promoter buying shares on the open market; and a demerger rationale (separating Mobility into its own listed entity) that is procedurally on track, if not yet finally approved.

What's not good: free cash flow has been negative for four straight years, and the FY26 cause — receivable days nearly tripling to 149 — is independently flagged by the company's own auditor as an impairment risk on 58.3% of total assets, a check no analyst on either call pushed back on; a contingent-liability guarantee to the Wind subsidiary grew 11x in a single year, sitting just outside the "net debt-free" narrative; the Wind segment posted an EBITDA loss in its first full commercial quarter, directly undercutting a "better than industry margin" claim made weeks earlier; the ash-volume ramp guidance has already been walked back once, from "this year" to "Q4FY27"; the Chairman/MD carries a ~41% pledge on his promoter stake and a SEBI UPSI finding (under stay); an income-tax search remains publicly unexplained beyond management's own assurance; institutional/analyst scrutiny is close to zero; and fully consolidated reporting won't arrive until Q4FY27, limiting today's comparability.

How management has navigated it: with a mix of real candor and some promotional smoothing. On the credit side: management disclosed the Wind EBITDA miss and its one-time refinancing-charge explanation plainly, corrected an internally inconsistent investor-deck figure within two hours of the original filing, and gave a specific, checkable timeline for the pledge unwind rather than a vague reassurance. On the debit side: the single largest quantitative flag in this dataset — the receivables build the auditor itself is watching — was addressed only in passing as "normal unbilled revenue," a moat question on Wind was answered with an unfalsifiable "competition is an opportunity," and the ash-volume target needed an in-call revision once an analyst pressed on the mismatch between the June framing and the August result.

AI-Synthesized ViewLikelihood the next 12 months resolve in the bulls' favour from today's starting price: moderate, roughly 40–50%. The core Ash & Coal business is demonstrably strong — profitable, order-book-backed, and growing well ahead of the multiple the market is currently paying for the stock (14.36x trailing, against 139%+ TTM revenue growth). But this is offset by a genuinely serious cash-conversion and receivables-quality question that the company's own auditor, not just this report, has flagged; by a Wind segment that is now the single largest swing factor in the consolidated numbers and has already missed its own margin framing once; and by governance data points (the pledge, the SEBI finding, the tax search) that are individually manageable but collectively argue for a discount to a clean-hands growth story. The base case above implies meaningful upside if Refex simply executes to its own restated guidance — but "restated" is the operative word, and the bear case is not a remote tail risk given how many of its inputs (receivables, the Venwind guarantee, Wind margin timing) are already visible, unresolved items in the FY26 numbers rather than hypotheticals.
